EU Blue Card

The following checklist (developed with the latest information available as of May 2024) serves as a tool for highly qualified workers from India to work in a European Union Member State under the EU Blue Card scheme.

The Blue Card is an EU-wide work and residence permit (Directive (EU) 2021/1883) that allows highly qualified non-EU citizens to work and live in 25 of the 27 Member States of the European Union (it does not apply in Denmark and Ireland).

Purpose and geographical scope of the EU Blue Card

The EU Blue Card is designed to attract and retain highly qualified non-EU nationals in the EU. It is aimed at enhancing the EU’s competitiveness in the global race for talent and to help the EU fill labour and skills shortages with qualified professionals from outside the EU. Blue Card status carries benefits such as favourable family reunification rules, intra-EU mobility, equal treatment to EU nationals in certain areas, as well as easier access to EU long-term residence status.

Note: Despite the existence of a common EU Blue Card provision, EU Member States still have the authority to determine the number of non-EU nationals eligible to enter their territory under the Blue Card scheme.

It is important to note that the Blue Card is only applicable by EU Member States (except Denmark and Ireland); and as such, does not grant the right to enter or reside in non-EU Schengen countries (Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Liechtenstein) without additional visa or entry procedures.

Flexible requirements are also applicable for Information Technology (IT) professionals in select EU Member States. It is best to check with the authorities of the respective Member State on your eligibility for these provisions before you make an application.

Application procedure

You – or your employer – must make your application to the competent national authorities in the country where you wish to work under the EU Blue Card scheme or, if applicable, at the consular authorities of the Member State where you wish to apply, in India.

Note: There is no uniform EU-wide application process for the EU Blue Card. Application submission procedures, including fees and payment modalities, differ across EU Member States. Normally, these country-specific application procedures and information can be found on the immigration portal of the respective EU Member State. For country-specific information, please refer to the links under “National immigration portals” below.

The updated EU Blue Card Directive specifies that a decision or response to an application must be provided within 90 days from the date of application submission. For applications submitted by “recognised employers” in dedicated Member States, this period is reduced to a maximum of 30 days.

The application at a glance

  1. 1 Preparatory phase

    Gather the necessary documents

    • Valid Indian passport (with minimum validity over 6 months)
    • Application for a valid visa, or a valid visa or residence permit
    • Legally binding job offer

    For the additional list of documents, please refer to the EU Immigration Portal and the national immigration portal of the respective Member State (links below).

  2. 2 Application phase

    Review the eligibility criteria

    • Higher educational qualification (diploma or degree)
    • or
    • At least 5 years of professional work experience (same sector)

    Then prepare the application

    • Check that your annual gross salary meets the threshold of the Member State
    • Consult the national immigration portal for any additional requirements
    • Secure medical insurance
    • Submit the application to the competent national authorities, or at the consulate in India

  3. 3 Decision phase

    • Wait for the decision, within 90 days
    • if approved
    • Receive the EU Blue Card

Common reasons for rejection or withdrawal of the Blue Card

  • False or incorrect information: Ensure all information and documents you submit are correct and genuine. False or misleading details will lead to application rejection.
  • Failure to meet the eligibility criteria: Ensure that you have a recognised university degree or relevant professional experience. Similarly, make sure you meet the salary threshold required to be eligible for a Blue Card. If you do not meet these criteria, your application will be rejected.

Additional note: Member States may also conduct a labour market test as per Article 7(2) to determine the availability of suitable EU candidates before approving your Blue Card application.

Benefits as an EU Blue Card holder

Validity of the Blue Card

The Blue Card is valid for at least 24 months, or, if a work contract is for a shorter period, a further 3 months after the contract ends.

Benefits of the Blue Card

  • Family reunification

    Blue Card holders can be joined by their family members, who are also granted the right to work. The years of residence count towards an autonomous residence permit and EU long-term residence status for the holder and their family, including spouses and minor children.

  • Increased intra-EU mobility

    After 12 months, holders can move with their families to another Member State without resetting their residence period. The application for a Blue Card there must be made no later than 1 month after entering that Member State.

  • Equal treatment

    The same rights as EU nationals for employment conditions, freedom of association, education and vocational training, recognition of diplomas, social security, and access to goods and services.

  • Long-term EU residency

    Holders can acquire EU long-term resident status, subject to certain conditions, which further stabilises their residence and work rights within the EU.
