Frequently asked questions

Answers for EU employers and institutions, and for Indian ICT professionals and students.

Answers to common questions from EU employers and institutions, and from Indian ICT professionals and students. They are general guidance, not legal advice: rules vary by country, institution and individual circumstances and may change, so always check the official sources. Cannot find your question? Contact us.

For employers and institutions

Why consider India for ICT talent?

India is one of the world's largest suppliers of ICT talent. India produces a very large number of graduates in computer science, data science, engineering, information technology, mathematics, and related fields each year. India’s skilled ICT workforce provides employers with access to diverse expertise across a range of technology fields.

How strong is the ICT education system in India?

India has a well-established higher education ecosystem for technology and engineering. Graduates come from Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), National Institutes of Technology (NITs), Indian Institutes of Information Technology (IIITs), Public and private universities, and Engineering colleges. In addition, a growing network of skilling agencies, technology academies, and industry-led training programmes plays an important role in advancing skills development and supporting upskilling and reskilling across the ICT sector and related industries.

Are Indian ICT professionals proficient in English?

Yes. English is widely used in Indian higher education, particularly in engineering, computer science, and technology programs. Most ICT professionals are comfortable communicating in English in international work environments.

What is the European Legal Gateway Office in India?

The European Legal Gateway Office in India is an EU-funded initiative that helps Indian ICT professionals, students, and researchers explore work, study, and research opportunities in European Union countries. It also supports EU employers by providing information, outreach activities, and connections to India's ICT talent ecosystem, helping facilitate safe and informed mobility through legal pathways.

How can the European Legal Gateway Office in India help EU employers hire ICT talent from India?

The European Legal Gateway Office in India will help EU employers identify and recruit skilled ICT professionals from India. Its support will include connecting EU employers with Indian talent pools, providing labour market intelligence, facilitating recruitment outreach and job fairs, building partnerships with relevant stakeholders, including universities, and offering practical guidance on international recruitment and compliance.

What types of EU stakeholders can work with the European Legal Gateway?

The initiative is open to EU employers and other relevant stakeholders, including chambers of commerce, ICT employer associations, sectoral platforms, SMEs, and growth-oriented technology firms. Stakeholders interested in becoming a partner can reach out to the EU Support Office in Brussels at eulgoindia.info [at] icmpd.org to express their interest and learn more about partnership opportunities.

Is there a cost to participate in the European Legal Gateway initiative?

No. This service is offered free of charge to both employers and candidates. No placement, recruitment, or access fees are required to participate.

How can EU employers assess the equivalence of Indian qualifications?

Indian academic qualifications can be formally compared through your country's national ENIC-NARIC centre. The ENIC-NARIC is part of a joint European Commission, Council of Europe, and UNESCO network of qualification recognition centres, operating under the principles of the Lisbon Recognition Convention (1997). You can find your country's centre here.

What legal pathway would we use to actually hire someone?

This depends on your country and the role. The most common route for skilled non-EU professionals is the EU Blue Card, a work and residence permit for highly qualified talent, valid in 25 of the 27 EU Member States. Full eligibility criteria and country-specific application details are available on the European Commission's EU Immigration Portal.

Does the EU Blue Card apply to every EU country?

No, Denmark and Ireland do not participate. Some countries also offer their own national permits for highly skilled workers alongside the Blue Card. Check your national authority's page via the EU Immigration Portal for the exact route that applies to you.

How long does the process typically take, from shortlisting to a candidate arriving?

Timelines vary by country and permit type, but several EU Member States now offer fast-track processing for highly skilled roles, in some cases a matter of weeks once a job offer is confirmed.

Can smaller companies and startups participate in this initiative, or is it only for large employers?

Yes. The initiative is open to employers of all sizes, including SMEs and startups.

Does this cover only full-time jobs, or also internships and research roles?

The Gateway supports a range of mobility opportunities for ICT professionals, students, and researchers, including employment, study, research, and, where available, internship opportunities.

Can EU employers participate in outreach or recruitment events in India?

Yes. The Gateway organises and supports employer missions, job fairs, outreach activities, and engagement with universities and other talent sources across India. These activities provide opportunities for EU employers, including SMEs, to engage directly with Indian ICT professionals, students, researchers, and educational institutions.

What specific skills or roles does this initiative focus on?

The Gateway focuses on the ICT sector, supporting opportunities for ICT professionals, students, and researchers. This includes digital and technology-related fields such as artificial intelligence, data science, cybersecurity, and other areas of growing demand across the EU.

Do you offer any pre-departure guidance for candidate?

Yes. The Gateway provides information and guidance to help ICT professionals, students, and researchers prepare for mobility to the EU. This may include information on legal pathways, living and working in Europe, and practical considerations related to relocation and integration.

For candidates: working in the EU

About the tool

What is the European Legal Gateway digital tool and what can it do for me?

It is a free online tool from the EU–India Legal Gateway that helps you understand the legal routes to work, study or do research in the EU. It explains how the pathways work, helps you identify which may be relevant to your profile, and links you to the official EU or Member State source for each. It does not process applications or place candidates.

Can this tool get me a job in the EU?

No. The tool does not place candidates, hold vacancies or approach employers on your behalf. It explains how recruitment and work authorisation work, and points you to official job portals such as EURES and to employers' own careers pages.

Can this tool guarantee I will get a work permit or visa?

No. Decisions on visas and permits are made solely by the competent authorities of EU Member States. The tool indicates which pathways may be relevant and where to apply; it cannot assess your application or predict its outcome.

Is there a cost to use this tool or the European Legal Gateway service?

No. The tool and all Gateway services are free of charge. The Gateway will never ask you for payment. Official application fees are paid directly to the relevant authority, never to the Gateway or to any intermediary acting in its name.

How current is the information in this tool?

Content is reviewed regularly and each page shows the date it was last reviewed. Immigration rules change often and figures such as salary thresholds are usually updated at least once a year, so confirm the current requirement on the official source linked from each answer before you act on it.

What should I do if my question isn't answered here?

Contact the Gateway through the contact form or the Gateway email address. The team can help you find the right information and point you to the responsible authority. For a decision on your individual case, you will need to approach the competent national authority directly.

Finding employment in the EU

How can I find a job in the EU as an Indian ICT professional?

Start with the Gateway's country profiles and vacancy signposting, then apply directly through official job portals or employer websites. The Gateway itself does not place candidates, it points to appropriate channels.

Which ICT occupations are currently in demand in the EU?

Software development, data and cybersecurity roles are consistently flagged as shortage occupations across several member states, which is also why ICT sits at the centre of this pilot.

Can I apply for jobs in the EU while I am still living in India?

Yes. EU employers and job portals accept applications from candidates based abroad, and recruitment normally takes place before you relocate. Whether you need an offer in hand before you can move is covered under Eligibility for an EU job.

Can an EU employer hire me directly from India?

Yes, provided the employer sponsors the relevant visa or work permit once an offer is made. Direct hiring from outside the EU is common in shortage occupations such as ICT, which is the basis for programmes like the EU Talent Pool and for this Gateway.

How do I know whether an EU job vacancy is genuine?

Genuine vacancies appear on the employer's own careers page or a recognised portal, come from a company email domain, and never ask you for payment. See Recruitment scams for the full checklist.

Can I apply for multiple EU countries at the same time?

Yes, there's no restriction on applying to several countries in parallel; just note that visa and recognition requirements differ by country, so track them separately.

Which EU country is best for my ICT profession?

There's no single best country; it depends on demand for the specific role, salary levels, language environment, and personal priorities.

Can I get an ICT job in the EU with English only?

Often yes for the role itself, particularly at larger international employers. Local language still matters for daily life and for long-term residence or citizenship later on. See Language for the full picture.

Eligibility for an EU job

Can I work in the EU if I am an Indian citizen?

Yes, as a third-country national, subject to obtaining the relevant work permit or visa for the country and role.

What are the requirements for an Indian citizen to work in the EU?

Generally: a valid job offer or qualifying pathway, proof of qualifications and a visa/work permit application meeting the host country's conditions.

Do I need a job offer before I can move to the EU for work?

In most cases yes. Some countries offer job-seeker visas or opportunity cards that allow entry to search for work, but these are the exception, not the default route.

Can I move to the EU first and then look for a job?

Only through specific job-seeker visa schemes where they exist; this is not the standard route and needs to be checked per country.

Do I need a university degree to work in the EU?

Not always. Several countries and the EU Blue Card framework allow relevant professional experience to substitute for a degree, especially in IT.

Can I work in the EU based on my professional experience instead of a degree?

Yes, this is explicitly recognised in ICT under several national schemes and the Blue Card directive, though the amount of experience required varies by country.

How many years of work experience do I need to work in the EU?

Varies by country and pathway; some Blue Card provisions accept experience-based qualification with several years of relevant professional experience.

Are there special pathways for highly skilled ICT professionals?

Yes. The EU Blue Card is the main EU-wide pathway for highly skilled roles, and several countries also run national skilled-worker and shortage-occupation schemes. See EU Blue Card for how it works and who qualifies.

Does my occupation qualify for a skilled-worker pathway?

Most ICT roles do, either under general skilled-worker rules or shortage-occupation provisions.

Are there shortage-occupation pathways for ICT professionals?

Yes, several countries apply lower salary thresholds or simplified procedures for shortage occupations, which frequently include ICT roles.

Can I work in the EU as a freelancer or self-employed ICT professional?

Some countries offer self-employment or freelance visas, but the requirements (business plan, minimum income, registration) differ substantially by country and are separate from employee work permits.

Work permits and visas

Do I need a work permit to work in an EU country?

Yes, as a non-EU national, unless a specific exemption applies.

What type of visa do I need to work in the EU?

Depends on the country and role commonly a national work visa, EU Blue Card or a specific skilled-worker permit.

What is the difference between a work visa, work permit and residence permit?

A visa is entry permission, a work permit authorises employment, and a residence permit authorises longer-term stay; many EU countries combine work and residence into one permit, but terminology and process still vary by country.

Who applies for my work permit – me or my employer?

Usually a shared process: the employer often initiates approval with national labour authorities, while the candidate applies for the visa at the relevant embassy or consulate.

Does my employer have to sponsor my work permit?

In most skilled-worker routes yes, the employer's job offer and in some countries, formal sponsorship is a prerequisite.

How long does it take to obtain a work permit?

Commonly several weeks, depending on document completeness and the processing country.

How much does a work permit application cost?

Fees are set nationally and change periodically.

What documents do I need for a work permit?

Typically a valid passport, signed job contract or offer letter, proof of qualifications, and sometimes proof of accommodation or funds.

Do I need a job contract before applying for a work permit?

Yes, in almost all skilled-worker and Blue Card routes a signed contract or binding offer is required.

Can my employer apply for my work permit before I arrive in the EU?

Yes, employer-side approval steps (such as labour market checks) typically happen before the candidate applies for the entry visa.

Can my work permit be refused?

Yes, if eligibility, salary, or documentation requirements aren't met, or on other national grounds.

What happens if my work permit application is rejected?

Depends on the specific reason and country procedure.

How long is my work permit valid?

Commonly one to four years depending on contract length and permit type.

Can I renew my work permit?

Yes, provided you still meet the underlying conditions (employment, salary threshold, etc.).

What happens to my residence permit if I lose my job?

Most countries provide a grace period in which to find new qualifying employment before your status is affected; the exact duration is set nationally. The same principle applies to EU Blue Card holders and to long-term residents, with the differences noted on those pages.

EU Blue Card

What is the EU Blue Card and who can apply?

A fast-track residence and work permit for skilled non-EU nationals with a qualifying job offer and salary; available to Indian ICT professionals meeting the criteria.

Can an Indian ICT professional apply for an EU Blue Card?

Yes, ICT is a commonly qualifying sector, including through the experience-based route in several countries.

What salary do I need for an EU Blue Card?

Set as a multiple of the host country's national average salary, so it differs by country.

Does the salary threshold differ between EU countries?

Yes

Do I need a university degree for an EU Blue Card?

Not necessarily. Recognised professional experience can substitute for a degree in several countries, particularly for ICT roles, subject to a minimum number of years of relevant experience under the revised Blue Card framework.

How long is an EU Blue Card valid?

Typically one to four years depending on the contract and country, and renewable while conditions are met.

Which EU countries issue EU Blue Cards?

Most EU Member States issue it. Ireland and Denmark operate their own national schemes instead and do not participate in the Blue Card.

Can I bring my family with me on an EU Blue Card?

Yes, family reunification is generally simplified for Blue Card holders compared with other permit types.

Can my spouse work if I have an EU Blue Card?

In most countries yes, the spouse receives labour market access without needing a separate work permit.

Can I change employers with an EU Blue Card?

After the first 12 months, generally yes without restriction, provided the new role still meets the salary threshold. During the first 12 months a change may need approval. For the general rules on changing employer, see Changing jobs and career mobility.

Can an EU Blue Card lead to permanent residence?

Yes, Blue Card holders often reach EU long-term resident status faster than other permit categories in some countries, though the standard EU-wide threshold is five years of legal continuous residence.

Can I move from one EU country to another with an EU Blue Card?

After roughly 12 months in the first country, Blue Card holders can move to a second participating country, but must still apply for a new Blue Card there.

What happens if I lose my job while holding an EU Blue Card?

The same grace-period principle applies as for other work permits: commonly a few months to find new qualifying employment, with the exact period set nationally. See Work permits and visas for the general rule.

Salary and employment conditions

Which EU countries offer the highest ICT salaries?

Western and northern European markets tend to pay more in absolute terms, but cost of living offsets thi

What is the difference between gross and net salary in the EU?

Gross is pay before tax and social security deductions; net is what is actually received. Deduction rates vary by country and income band.

How much will I take home after tax?

Depends on gross salary, country, and personal circumstances

Are salaries negotiable in EU jobs?

Generally yes, particularly for skilled and shortage-occupation roles, though norms around negotiation differ by country and company culture.

What benefits do EU employers typically provide?

Commonly health insurance contributions, pension contributions, paid leave, and sometimes relocation support; specifics vary by country and employer.

How many hours do ICT professionals normally work in the EU?

Full-time is commonly around 35–40 hours per week across most EU countries, though this varies slightly by country and sector.

How much annual leave am I entitled to?

EU law sets a minimum of 4 weeks paid annual leave; several countries provide more.

What are my rights as an employee in the EU?

EU and national labour law cover working time, leave, non-discrimination, health and safety, and dismissal protections

What happens if I am dismissed from my job?

Notice periods and protections apply under national labour law.

What is the notice period for employment in the EU?

Set by national law and often also by contract terms.

Am I entitled to social security and healthcare as a foreign worker?

Generally yes, once legally employed and registered, foreign workers are covered under the same social security and healthcare systems as nationals in most EU countries.

Qualifications and recognition

Is my Indian degree recognised in the EU?

Recognition is assessed case by case and country by country; there is no blanket EU-wide recognition of foreign degrees.

Do I need to have my Indian degree recognised before applying for an EU job?

Not always before applying, but often before starting work in regulated professions, or before the work permit is finalised.

How can I get my Indian qualification recognised?

Through the relevant national recognition authority, and via ENIC-NARIC for comparability assessments across countries.

Which authority recognises foreign qualifications in each EU country?

Each country has its own designated body.

How long does qualification recognition take?

Commonly weeks to a few months depending on the country and profession.

How much does qualification recognition cost?

Fees are set nationally and by the specific recognition body.

Does my Indian university need to be accredited for my qualification to be recognised?

Recognition bodies typically check that the awarding institution is recognised under the Indian higher education system (e.g. UGC-recognised); assessed as part of the recognition procedure.

Is professional recognition different from academic qualification recognition?

Yes. Academic recognition compares your degree to a local equivalent; professional recognition (needed for regulated professions like law) authorises you to actually practise.

Is my ICT profession regulated in the EU country I want to work in?

Most ICT roles are not formally regulated professions in most EU countries, unlike law or medicine, but this should be confirmed per country and role.

Do software developers need professional licensing in the EU?

Generally no, software development is not a licensed profession in most EU countries, unlike regulated fields such as law or engineering in some jurisdictions.

Language

Do I need to know the local language to work in the EU?

Not always for the job itself, especially in ICT, but it matters for daily life, integration, and long-term residence or citizenship requirements.

Can I get an ICT job in Europe with English only?

Yes, in many companies and countries, particularly larger international employers.

What language level is required for employment?

Varies by employer and role; some ICT employers only require English, others may ask for basic local language skills for team integration.

Do I need a language certificate?

Only if the job or visa route specifically requires it; the Blue Card itself generally does not mandate a language certificate for entry, though permanent residence often does.

Are language requirements different for different ICT occupations?

Not usually role-specific within ICT, but client-facing or regulated roles are more likely to require local language proficiency than purely technical ones.

Can my employer require me to learn the local language after I arrive?

Yes, employers can set this as a condition of continued employment or integration, though this should be clarified in the contract before accepting the offer.

Applying for jobs

How should I prepare my CV for an EU employer?

Keep it concise, in the format expected by the target country, and tailored to the role; the Europass CV format is widely recognised across the EU and a safe default.

Is there a standard European CV format?

Yes, Europass is the EU's standard CV format and is accepted across all member states, though individual employers may still have their own preferences.

Should I use Europass when applying for ICT jobs?

It's a reasonable default, especially when applying across multiple countries, though check individual employer preferences too.

Do EU employers require a cover letter?

Often yes, particularly for roles outside large multinational tech firms; requirements vary by employer and country.

What documents should I submit with my job application?

Typically CV, cover letter, and sometimes copies of qualifications or certifications; specific requirements vary by employer.

Do I need to provide references from India?

Often expected, especially for senior roles; check the specific employer's process.

Will my Indian work experience be recognised by EU employers?

Yes, professional experience is generally valued by employers directly, separate from the formal degree-recognition process required for immigration purposes.

Do EU employers conduct background checks?

Commonly yes, particularly for larger companies, though scope and process vary by country and employer.

What should I expect during an EU job interview?

Structured interviews are common, sometimes including technical assessments for ICT roles; practices vary by company and country.

Are technical interviews different in EU companies?

Broadly similar to global tech industry practice (coding tests, technical rounds), though format and emphasis vary by employer.

How long does the recruitment process usually take?

Varies too much by employer and country to generalise reliably.

How can I verify whether a recruitment agency is legitimate?

Check it against official licensing registers, confirm it never charges candidates a placement fee, and cross-check company details independently. See Recruitment scams for the full verification checklist.

Can I be charged a recruitment fee for an EU job?

No. Legitimate recruitment never charges a candidate to be placed in a job. See Recruitment scams.

Who should pay recruitment or visa-related costs?

In most legitimate arrangements, the employer covers recruitment costs; the candidate typically covers their own visa application and travel unless the employer explicitly offers relocation support.

Relocation after getting a job

What are the steps from receiving a job offer to moving to the EU?

Broadly: sign the contract, complete or confirm qualification recognition, apply for the visa or work permit, arrange travel and initial accommodation, then complete registration on arrival. Sequence and timing are country-specific, and the permit and recognition steps can often run in parallel.

When should I apply for my visa/residence permit?

As soon as the signed contract and required documents are ready; processing times mean earlier is better.

What documents should I prepare before travelling?

Passport, visa/residence permit, signed contract, proof of accommodation, health insurance proof, and qualification documents are commonly required.

How much money should I have before relocating?

Some countries set a minimum proof-of-funds requirement as part of the visa application.

How can I find accommodation after moving to the EU?

Through national housing portals, relocation services, or employer support where offered.

How much does it cost to relocate from India to the EU?

Depends heavily on destination, visa fees, and personal circumstances.

Do employers provide relocation assistance?

Some do, particularly larger companies hiring internationally; this varies by employer and should be clarified during offer negotiation.

Can my employer pay for my relocation?

Yes, this is a negotiable benefit in many offers, though not guaranteed.

How do I open a bank account after moving to the EU?

Typically requires proof of address, residence permit, and passport; process and requirements are country-specific.

How do I access healthcare after moving for work?

Once registered as an employee, most countries provide access to statutory health insurance; some visa categories require private health cover for the initial application period.

Do I have to pay social security contributions?

Generally yes, as an employee in most EU countries, contributions are compulsory and shared between employer and employee.

Will my Indian pension/social-security contributions be recognised?

India has social security agreements with some EU countries that allow contribution totalisation or exemption; this depends on whether the specific country has such an agreement with India and should be confirmed.

Family reunification

Can I bring my spouse to the EU if I get a job?

Yes, generally through family reunification provisions, with simplified conditions for Blue Card holders in particular.

Can I bring my children with me?

Yes, minor children are generally covered under family reunification rules.

Can my spouse work in the EU?

In most countries yes, particularly under Blue Card family reunification, spouses typically receive labour market access without needing a separate permit.

How long does family reunification take?

Processing times vary; Blue Card family permits are meant to be processed alongside the main applicant's where conditions are met.

What are the income requirements for bringing my family?

Many countries require proof of sufficient income to support family members, with thresholds set nationally.

Do I need to provide accommodation for my family before they join me?

Often yes, proof of adequate housing is a common requirement, though Blue Card cases sometimes have relaxed conditions here.

Can my parents join me in the EU?

Generally more restricted than spouse/child reunification; some countries only allow this in exceptional hardship cases.

What healthcare rights does my family have?

Family members who join under reunification typically gain access to the same statutory health system as the sponsoring worker, once registered.

Can my children attend school in the EU?

Yes, children of legally resident workers generally have the right to attend public schooling.

Changing jobs and career mobility

Can I change employers after moving to the EU?

Yes, generally, though the first months on some permits require you to notify or seek approval from the immigration authority.

Do I need to inform immigration authorities if I change jobs?

Often yes, at least during an initial period; requirements vary by country and permit type.

What happens to my residence permit if I change employers?

It usually remains valid as long as the new role still meets the conditions of the original permit (salary, occupation type). If it does not, you may need an amendment or a new permit — a change of job does not automatically require a new permit, but it does require checking against your permit's conditions.

Can I move from an ICT job to another occupation?

Possible, but the new role needs to still satisfy the conditions of your existing permit or you may need to apply for a different permit category.

Can I move from one EU Member State to another for work?

Not automatically with a national permit, which is generally valid only in the country that issued it. EU Blue Card holders have a simplified route after about 12 months, but a fresh application in the new country is still required.

Can I work remotely for an employer in another EU country?

This raises tax and social security questions in both countries and isn't simply covered by your existing work permit.

Can I become self-employed after moving to the EU?

Some countries allow a switch to a self-employment or freelance permit, but this is a separate application with its own conditions. See Eligibility for an EU job for the requirements.

Long-term residence and settlement

Can I obtain permanent residence after working in the EU?

Yes, through the EU Long-Term Residents Directive or national permanent residence schemes.

How many years do I need to live in an EU country before applying for permanent residence?

Five years of legal, continuous residence is the standard EU-wide threshold under the Long-Term Residents Directive; some national schemes or Blue Card provisions offer faster routes in specific countries.

Can working in the EU eventually lead to citizenship?

Yes, in principle, though citizenship is governed entirely by national law, not EU law, and requirements (years of residence, language, civics tests) vary widely by country.

How long does it take to qualify for citizenship?

Varies significantly by country, commonly longer than the five years needed for EU long-term residence.

What happens to my residence status if I lose my job?

Most work-based permits allow a grace period in which to find new qualifying employment — see Work permits and visas for the general rule. Long-term resident status, once granted, is more secure and is not tied to a single job.

Can I return to India temporarily while holding an EU work permit?

Yes, but absences of more than roughly six consecutive months (or ten months cumulative within the qualifying period) can break continuity for long-term residence purposes, so this needs care.

Can I bring my family permanently after obtaining long-term residence?

Yes, family members already reunified generally retain their status, and long-term residence strengthens the overall stability of the family's position.

Recruitment scams

How can I identify a fake EU job offer?

Red flags include: unsolicited contact via WhatsApp or personal email, no verifiable company website or registered address, requests for payment at any stage, offers with no interview, and poor grammar or generic company branding.

How can I verify an EU employer?

Go directly to the company's own official website (not a link the recruiter sent), check for the role on their own careers page, and cross-check company registration where possible rather than relying on documents the recruiter provides.

How can I verify a recruitment agency?

Check licensing status with the relevant authority, confirm it doesn't charge candidates a fee, and look for independent reviews or a verifiable track record rather than agency-provided references only.

Should I pay money to an agent to get an EU job?

No. Legitimate recruitment never requires the candidate to pay for placement, visa processing or registration.

Can an employer ask me to pay for recruitment?

No. In most destination countries it is illegal for employers to charge recruitment costs to the worker; any such request is a strong scam indicator.

How do I identify a fake work permit or visa?

Verify any permit or visa document directly with the issuing country's embassy or official immigration portal rather than trusting the document or the agent alone.

What should I do if I think I have been scammed?

Stop all communication and payment immediately, do not share further personal or financial documents, and report it through official channels.

Where can I report an illegal recruitment agency?

In India, this can be reported to the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or the National Cyber Helpline (1930) for financial fraud, alongside any relevant state recruitment or emigration authority.

Where can I get help if I face an employment problem in an EU country?

The Indian embassy or consulate in that country and host-country labour authorities.

For students

These answers provide general guidance for Indian candidates. Admission, fees, scholarships, visa, residence and work rules vary by country, institution and individual circumstances and may change. Always verify the current requirements through the relevant university and official national or EU sources before applying, paying or travelling.

Finding the right programme

What kinds of ICT programmes can I study in the EU?

Universities and other recognised higher education institutions in EU Member States offer ICT-related bachelor's, master's and doctoral programmes, as well as specialised courses, in areas such as computer science, software engineering, data science, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, telecommunications and digital technologies. Options vary by country and institution.

How do I find an ICT programme that matches my background?

Start by checking the programme’s subject area, entry requirements, curriculum, language of instruction, duration and tuition fees. You should also check whether your previous qualification is accepted for that programme.

Can I study in the EU after completing my bachelor’s degree in India?

Yes. Your eligibility will depend on the qualification you hold, the programme you choose and the admission rules of the institution. Some institutions may require an assessment or recognition of your previous qualification.

Can I apply for a master’s programme if my bachelor’s degree is in a related but different subject?

Sometimes. Universities decide whether your previous academic background meets the programme requirements. Check the required subjects, credits or prior knowledge before applying.

Can I apply while I am still in my final year?

Some institutions allow final-year students to apply, while some may require your degree as proof before enrolment. Check the programme's specific admission conditions and document deadlines.

Where can I check whether my Indian qualification is recognised?

Recognition procedures differ between countries and institutions. Use the official qualification-recognition or university resources for the country and institution you are applying to. The final admission decision normally rests with the institution.

Some courses may also specify a required European Qualifications Framework (EQF) level or another national qualification level. You can use the Europass Compare Qualifications tool to understand how qualification levels in European countries relate to the EQF.

Do I need to take an entrance examination?

It depends on the programme. Some programmes admit students on the basis of their academic record and documents, while others may require an entrance test, interview, portfolio or other assessment.

Do I need work experience for an ICT master’s programme?

Not necessarily. Many master’s programmes focus on academic qualifications rather than work experience, but some programmes may require or prefer professional experience. Check the individual programme requirements.

Language

Can I study an ICT programme in English?

Yes. Many European institutions offer programmes taught fully or partly in English, particularly at master’s and doctoral level. Always check the language of instruction for the individual programme.

Do I need to know the local language to study in the EU?

Not always. Many European universities offer programmes taught entirely or partly in English. If your programme is taught in English and you meet its language requirements, you may not need to know the local language for your studies. However, learning the local language can be helpful for everyday life, social integration and employment.

Do I need and English Proficiency test like IELTS or TOEFL?

Many English-taught programmes require proof of English proficiency, but the exact requirement varies. Some institutions may accept other tests or exemptions in certain circumstances. Check the programme’s official requirements.

What English score do I need?

There is no single EU-wide score requirement. The university and programme decide the required level and accepted tests.

Can I learn the local language after I arrive?

In many countries, universities and other institutions offer language courses. Check what your host institution or local authorities provide.

Application process

How do I apply to a university in the EU?

The application process depends on the country and institution. You may apply directly to the university or through a national or centralised application platform. Always follow the instructions given by the institution.

What documents do I usually need for a university application?

Common documents include your passport, academic certificates and transcripts, proof of language proficiency, CV and motivation letter. Some programmes may also require references, portfolios, entrance-test results or other documents. Always check with the institution offering the course.

Do I need a motivation letter?

Many programmes ask for one, particularly at master’s and doctoral level. It normally explains your academic background, interests, motivation for choosing the programme and your future plans. Check the individual programme requirements.

Do I need a CV to apply for a master’s programme?

Some universities require a CV and others do not. If requested, it should focus on your education, relevant projects, internships, research and professional experience.

How early should I start my application?

Start preparing well before the application deadline, ideally several months in advance. You may need time to gather documents, take language tests and complete other admission requirements. If you are admitted, you will then need additional time for visa or residence procedures before your course starts.

When are university application deadlines?

There is no single EU-wide deadline. Deadlines vary by country, university, programme and intake. Always check the official programme website.

Can I apply to more than one university?

In many cases, yes. However, application rules and fees vary, so check the conditions of each institution or application system.

Can I apply directly to the university or do I need an agent?

Many European universities allow international students to apply directly. An education agent is not always necessary. Use the university’s official application instructions and be cautious about paying third parties for services that the university provides directly.

What happens after I submit my application?

The institution will assess your application and may request additional documents, an interview or an entrance assessment. If successful, you will receive an admission or offer letter explaining the next steps.

Tuition fees and funding

Is studying in the EU free for Indian students?

Not generally. Tuition fees vary widely between countries, institutions and programmes. Some programmes have low or no tuition fees, while others can be expensive. Always check the official university fee information.

How much does it cost to study in Europe?

Your total cost usually includes tuition, accommodation, food, transport, insurance, visa/residence costs and other personal expenses. Costs vary significantly by country and city.

Are there scholarships for Indian students?

Yes. Scholarships may be offered by the EU, individual Member States, universities and other organisations. Eligibility and application deadlines vary. Here are some of the main places to find European higher education scholarships and funding opportunities. You can also visit the country profiles to find out more about scholarship opportunities per country.

Can I get a fully funded scholarship?

Some scholarships may cover tuition and provide support towards living or other costs, but not every scholarship is fully funded. Check exactly what each scholarship covers before applying.

Where can I find scholarships?

Start with official EU, Member State, university and scholarship-provider websites. Do not assume that every scholarship advertised online is genuine. Here are some of the main places to find European higher education scholarships and funding opportunities. You can also visit the country profiles to find out more about scholarship opportunities per country.

Can I get a scholarship after receiving university admission?

Sometimes. Some scholarships require a university offer first, while others have a separate application process or allow you to apply before admission. Check the scholarship rules carefully.

Can I take an education loan to study in Europe?

Education loans may be available through banks or other financial institutions in India. Eligibility and conditions vary. A university admission letter does not guarantee that you will receive a loan.

Do I need to prove that I have enough money to study in Europe?

In many cases of self-funded programmes, yes. You may need to demonstrate that you can support yourself during your stay, particularly as part of the visa or residence process. The amount and accepted forms of financial proof vary by country.

Visa and residence

Do I need a visa to study in the EU?

As an Indian citizen, you will generally need the appropriate visa or residence authorisation to study in an EU Member State. The type of authorisation and application procedure depend on the country, duration and type of study. Requirements and procedures vary by country, so always check the official immigration or embassy website of your destination country.

Do I need a short-stay or long-stay visa?

This depends mainly on how long you will study and the rules of your destination country. For short study periods of up to 90 days within a 180-day period, Schengen visa rules may apply in the Schengen Area. If your studies last more than 90 days, you will generally need to apply for a national long-stay visa and/or student residence permit in the country where you will study.

What is the Schengen Area, and does a Schengen visa allow me to study in the EU?

The Schengen Area is a group of 29 European countries that have common rules for short stays of up to 90 days within any 180-day period. A Schengen visa generally allows travel within the Schengen Area during its validity.

However, a Schengen visa is not normally the appropriate authorisation for a long-term study programme. If your studies last more than 90 days, you will generally need a national long-stay visa and/or student residence permit for the country where you will study. The requirements and procedures vary by country. For more information, see the European Commission's Visa Policy.

Where do I apply for my student visa?

Apply through the competent embassy, consulate or authorised visa application centre for your destination country. Always use the official government or diplomatic website to identify the correct procedure.

When should I apply for my visa?

Start as early as possible after you have the required admission documents or at least 2–3 months before your programme begins. You may need to attend an appointment or interview at an embassy, consulate or visa application centre, and securing an appointment can take time.

You can also check with your university’s international office or admissions team, as they may provide guidance or direct you to useful visa resources. Always check the official visa requirements and processing timelines for your destination country. Visa procedures can take time, and some countries recommend applying several months before travel.

What documents are usually required for a student visa?

Visa requirements vary by country, but you will generally need a valid passport, letter of admission from a recognised institution, proof of financial means, proof of accommodation, health insurance and a completed visa application form.

Depending on your destination country and study programme, you may also need additional documents, such as language test results, medical certificates, proof of tuition fee payment or other declarations. Always check the official visa or immigration requirements of your destination country before applying to make sure you have all the required documents.

Can I enter Europe on a tourist visa and then change to a student visa?

You should not assume that this is possible. Immigration rules differ by country, and some countries do not allow a tourist status to be converted into student status. Obtain the correct authorisation before travelling.

Do I need a residence permit after arriving?

For longer study stays, you may need to obtain a student residence permit in addition to or following your entry visa. The procedure and deadlines depend on the destination country.

Can I travel to other EU countries with my student residence permit?

A student residence permit is generally linked to the country that issued it. Travel to other countries may be possible under Schengen or other mobility rules, but this does not automatically give you the right to study or reside there. Check the rules before travelling or starting studies elsewhere.

Accommodation, insurance and arrival

How do I find student accommodation?

Check whether your university offers student residences or housing support. You can also use official student housing platforms and reputable private providers. Start looking early, particularly in cities with limited housing.

Does the university provide accommodation?

Some institutions provide student residences, while others do not. Availability may also be limited, so admission does not necessarily guarantee a room.

Do I need health insurance?

Yes, health insurance is an important part of preparing for study in Europe, and it may be a legal requirement depending on your country of destination and residence status. Check the national requirements before travelling.

Can I use my Indian health insurance in Europe?

Do not assume that an Indian policy will meet the requirements of your destination country. Check whether it is recognised and whether it provides the required coverage.

What should I arrange before travelling to Europe?

Make sure you have your admission documents, visa/residence documents, insurance and accommodation arranged. Also check your university's arrival instructions and any registration requirements after arrival.

Working while studying

Can I work part-time while studying?

In many EU countries, international students may work subject to specific conditions. The permitted hours, type of work and any work-permit requirements depend on the country and your residence status.

How many hours can I work as a student?

There is no single EU-wide limit. Check the official rules of your destination country before taking up employment.

Can I use part-time work to fund my studies?

Part-time work can provide additional income, but you should not assume that it will cover your tuition and living costs. You may also need to show sufficient funds when applying for your visa or residence permit.

Can I work in an ICT job while studying?

Possibly, depending on the country, your residence status and the conditions attached to your studies. Any employment must comply with the relevant national rules.

After graduation

Can I stay in the EU after completing my studies?

Some countries offer options for graduates to remain temporarily to look for work or start another permitted activity. These rules vary by country and can change, so check the current national requirements.

Can I work in the EU after graduating?

You may be able to move from student status to an employment-based residence status if you meet the relevant national conditions and obtain a qualifying job or other required basis for residence. This is not automatic.

Does studying in one EU country allow me to work in another EU country after graduation?

No. A degree from one EU country does not automatically give you the right to work or reside in another Member State. You must meet the immigration and employment conditions of the country where you want to work.

Will an EU degree be recognised in India?

Recognition depends on the qualification, institution and purpose for which you need recognition in India. Check the relevant Indian authority or professional regulator where applicable.
