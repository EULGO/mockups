Glossary

Key terms used in legal mobility, employment and the recognition of qualifications in the European Union.

Audience
Candidates, employers and higher education institutions
Scope
Legal mobility to the European Union
  • Download PDF
  • Email

On this page

    Labour Market Test (LMT)

    A regulatory procedure requiring an employer in a destination State to demonstrate that a vacancy cannot be filled from the resident labour force before authorising the admission of a third-country national for employment. It serves as a preference-based admission safeguard conditioning the issuance of a work or single permit on evidenced domestic shortage. Requirements and application vary by Member State.

    Shortage Occupation List

    An instrument by which a destination State pre-identifies occupations for which demand cannot be met from the resident labour force within a reasonable period. Such a listing implies relaxed admission conditions, such as labour market test exemption, reduced salary thresholds, or fast-track processing. It shifts the demonstration of shortage from a per-vacancy employer burden to a standing occupation-level determination by the State. List content and criteria vary by country.

    ENIC-NARIC

    ENIC-NARIC is a network of national offices across Europe (and some countries beyond it) that deal with the recognition of foreign degrees and diplomas. If you studied in one country and want to work or study in another, the ENIC-NARIC centre in your destination country can inform you on how your qualification compares to its national qualifications. The name comes from two originally separate networks that now operate together: ENIC (European Network of Information Centres in the European Region), set up by the Council of Europe and UNESCO, and NARIC (National Academic Recognition Information Centres), set up by the European Commission. Further information is available at https://www.enic-naric.net/.

    Single Permit 

    A Single Permit is a combined residence and work permit that allows a non-EU citizen to live and work in an EU Member State through one application process. Instead of applying separately for a work permit and a residence permit, the person receives a single document that covers both. It gives non-EU workers the right to be treated equally with nationals of EU Member States in many respects including working conditions, such as pay, working hours, health and safety requirements, education and training, certain social security benefits, recognition of qualification and tax benefits. Single permit holders can change their employers during the permit’s validity, subject to relevant legislation in the Member States.
