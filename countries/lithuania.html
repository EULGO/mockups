In brief

Lithuania had about 77,600 ICT specialists in 2025 (6.7% of employment), with strong demand for selected mid-level and senior profiles in a small, selective ecosystem. Vilnius and Kaunas are the main hubs, 6,341 Indian nationals held work or high-skilled permits on 1 June 2026, and the EU Blue Card is the main route for highly qualified roles.

ICT mobility at a glance

Market signal: fast structural growth, selective current recruitment

Eurostat records Lithuania’s ICT-specialist share at 6.7% in 2025 and the fastest average annual increase in ICT-specialist employment in the EU over 2015–2025. The EULGO internal mapping nevertheless points to a compact market centred on experienced specialists. Candidates should assess specific vacancies and employer readiness rather than assume a universal shortage.

03Balanced candidate assessment

Why consider Lithuania?

Potential advantages

  • 6.7% ICT-specialist share of employment—above the EU average—and the EU’s fastest long-term ICT-specialist growth over 2015–2025.
  • Visible strengths in software, fintech, cybersecurity, cloud, data/AI and digital business services.
  • English-friendly international technology teams in Vilnius and Kaunas.
  • Current Blue Card thresholds are transparent and relatively moderate compared with some larger EU markets.
  • Existing Indian employment and education community, with established employers and universities.
  • Lower overall living costs than many western European technology hubs.

Things to consider

  • Small absolute labour market: even strong ICT intensity does not mean a large volume of vacancies.
  • Mapping demand is concentrated in mid-level and senior profiles; junior/generalist candidates may struggle.
  • Migration policy and employer controls have tightened, and country-specific application restrictions apply in India.
  • Lithuanian can be important beyond international teams and for long-term integration.
  • Salaries are lower than in many western/northern EU destinations, although living costs are also generally lower.
  • One university consultation reported weak retention of Indian graduates into Lithuanian ICT jobs; this is qualitative, institution-specific evidence.

01Demand, role families and verified benchmarks

Where are the ICT opportunities?

Lithuania has a small but highly digital and rapidly expanding technology ecosystem. ICT specialists represented 6.7% of total employment in 2025—above the EU average—and the country recorded the EU’s fastest average annual growth in ICT-specialist employment over 2015–2025. Software and IT services, fintech, telecommunications and digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, data and AI are central parts of the ecosystem.

The internal Lithuania mapping adds a demand-side view across 94 named companies. Software engineering appears most consistently, followed by data/AI, cloud/DevOps, infrastructure and cybersecurity. The mapped market is strongly oriented toward mid-level and senior specialists. These findings are directional intelligence from named employers, not a statistically representative national vacancy survey.

04What the 2026 Lithuania mapping suggests

Applying from India

  • Target roles where the match is clearest. The mapping shows the broadest demand for experienced software engineers and developers, followed by data/AI, cloud/DevOps, infrastructure/network, cybersecurity and specialised enterprise technology profiles such as SAP.
  • Demonstrate depth, not only technology keywords. Employers are more likely to relocate candidates who can show architecture decisions, production ownership, security or reliability outcomes, stakeholder communication and evidence of work in complex teams.
  • Address employer uncertainty directly. One consultation highlighted concerns about assessing large applicant volumes and supporting integration. This is a qualitative employer perception—not a general finding about Indian talent. Strong references, verifiable work samples, structured technical evidence and realistic relocation planning can reduce uncertainty.
  • Make language and integration readiness visible. English is common in internationally oriented technology companies, especially in Vilnius and Kaunas. Lithuanian can still be important for local clients, public-facing work, smaller employers and long-term integration.
  • Prepare the mobility file early. Keep passports, degrees, transcripts, detailed experience letters, employment contracts, police-clearance and civil-status documents ready. Confirm translation, legalisation, health-insurance and accommodation evidence for the specific route in MIGRIS.

Qualitative evidence: Internal EULGO Country Engagement Guidelines: Lithuania and LT ICT Sector Mapping Outline, 2026. Employer and programme findings are directional and should not be interpreted as representative survey statistics.

05

Recruitment: know your rights

India application point

Lithuania’s external service provider accepts temporary-residence-permit applications in New Delhi and Mumbai. The service fee is EUR 110 and appointments are booked only through MIGRIS after the application reaches the required status. Country-specific restrictions apply to which grounds can be submitted in India; verify eligibility before paying or travelling.

Before accepting an offer

  • Verify the employer, legal entity, work location, gross salary, probation, working time and benefits in a written contract.
  • Confirm the exact residence route and whether the employer will submit the mediation letter and support relocation.
  • Check whether salary meets the current BDU multiple and whether the occupation is on the relevant shortage/high-value list.
  • Clarify who pays state, VFS, translation, insurance and relocation costs.

Red flags

  • A recruiter guarantees approval of a visa or residence permit.
  • Payment is requested for a non-verifiable vacancy, contract or employer invitation.
  • The salary, employer, location or role differs between the offer and MIGRIS documentation.
  • You are advised to work before the correct permit/status is valid.
  • Your passport or original documents are retained without a lawful reason and receipt.

Higher education options & costs

Selected ICT-relevant institutions

  • Vilnius University (VU)
  • Kaunas University of Technology (KTU)
  • VILNIUS TECH
  • Vytautas Magnus University
  • Klaipėda University
  • Vilnius College / Kaunas College and other universities of applied sciences
  • TECHIN, Turing College, CodeAcademy and other mapped training providers

Study practicalities

  • The national portal indicates average annual tuition from about EUR 1,300 for bachelor’s, EUR 2,300 for master’s and EUR 8,400 for doctoral studies; specific ICT programmes may be higher.
  • English-taught computer science, software engineering, data/AI and cybersecurity programmes are available at leading institutions.
  • Working during studies: For permits issued after 22 May 2026, first- and second-year bachelor's students may work up to 20 hours/week (excluding holidays and practical training); later-year bachelor's, master's and doctoral students may work up to 40 hours/week.
  • After graduation, a 12-month residence permit may be requested for job search and self-employment.
  • Family accompaniment is generally not available for students, except doctoral students.
  • Indian applicants should confirm academic recognition, tuition, proof of funds and India-specific residence-permit access with the institution and MIGRIS.

The institution list reflects the internal EULGO Lithuania academic mapping and is not a ranking. Programme language, tuition, admission rules, qualification recognition and scholarships must be checked directly with the institution and the national Study in Lithuania portal.

From study to employment

  1. Complete eligible higher-education studies in Lithuania
  2. Apply for 12-month job-search / self-employment TRP
  3. Target a role matching skills and market demand
  4. Confirm whether the activity is authorised under current status
  5. Change to Blue Card or other work-based residence when required

Critical distinction for Indian graduates

Eligible graduates may apply through MIGRIS for a 12-month temporary residence permit for job search or self-employment after completing studies. The search permit is not a substitute for every employment authorisation: confirm whether the specific planned activity is permitted before commencing it. Once a suitable offer is secured, apply for the appropriate employment-based residence title, including the EU Blue Card where eligible. Check current Migration Department guidance before filing.

04

Research opportunities

Lithuania’s ICT-relevant research environment includes computer science, AI and data science, cybersecurity, informatics engineering, fintech, telecommunications and digital public innovation. Opportunities are concentrated in Vilnius University, Kaunas University of Technology, VILNIUS TECH, research institutes, technology centres and corporate R&D teams.

Find opportunities

  • EURAXESS Lithuania / EURAXESS Europe.
  • Vilnius University institutes and doctoral schools.
  • Kaunas University of Technology research groups and AI/data programmes.
  • VILNIUS TECH doctoral and informatics-engineering opportunities.
  • Research Council of Lithuania calls and funded projects.
  • Corporate R&D in fintech, cyber, telecom, software, data and digital services.

For researchers & doctoral candidates

  • The specific researcher route requires an employment contract with a research institution; a regular route can also use an employment or author’s contract in eligible cases.
  • Researchers using the specific procedure are exempt from a work permit and may teach according to Lithuanian law.
  • Immediate family reunification is available; family members can work without a separate work permit.
  • After research, a 12-month temporary residence permit can be requested for job search and self-employment.
  • Doctoral students can work without the standard 20-hour weekly limit and may be accompanied by family.

Frequently asked questions

Recognition of Indian qualifications, working in English and moving with your family in Lithuania.

For ICT professionals

  • Most software, data, cloud, cyber and general IT roles are non-regulated occupations; the employer normally assesses professional suitability.
  • Immigration eligibility can still require evidence of a higher-education qualification or qualifying professional experience.
  • For Blue Card ICT managers/specialists, at least 3 years’ relevant experience in the previous 7 years can meet the high-level-skills condition in qualifying cases.
  • Keep detailed employer letters describing dates, duties, seniority, technologies and full-time status.

Academic recognition / documents

  • SKVC and authorised higher-education institutions handle academic recognition depending on the purpose and applicant status.
  • For non-regulated employment, an employer can decide whether a foreign qualification is suitable; regulated professions have separate competent authorities.
  • Typical evidence includes final diploma, transcript, passport and prior qualifications; country-specific requirements may apply.
  • Documents not issued in Lithuanian or one of SKVC’s accepted languages generally require an official Lithuanian or English translation.

  • GuideRecognition of qualifications

English is a real working language in parts of the ICT market—but not the whole market

International technology, fintech, cybersecurity, product and global-business-services teams commonly use English. The mapping also shows substantial English-taught ICT education at university level. Lithuanian can nevertheless be required for domestic customers, public-sector interaction, local operations, smaller firms and many vocational programmes. Candidates should check the actual team language and state their willingness to learn Lithuanian.

Family mobility depends on the principal route

Blue Card holders, researchers and intra-corporate transferees can generally access immediate family reunification, and family members holding the relevant residence permit may work without a separate work permit. Ordinary employed workers do not have the same immediate entitlement and may be subject to general waiting and sponsor conditions. Most students cannot be accompanied by family, except doctoral students. Always verify the rule attached to the principal applicant’s permit.
