02

ICT mobility at a glance

Market signal: strong specialist demand, but not a blanket vacancy market

Austria’s ICT-specialist share reached 5.4% in 2025 versus 5.0% EU-wide. The Commission still identifies ICT shortages as a constraint, and Statistics Austria found that 82% of enterprises recruiting/trying to recruit ICT specialists cited too few applications. Yet software-developer job ads fell from 27,605 in 2024 to 20,948 in 2025. Candidates should therefore target verified specialist vacancies rather than assume automatic shortage-driven recruitment.

03Balanced candidate assessment

Why consider Austria?

Potential advantages

  • ICT specialists were 5.4% of employment in 2025, above the EU average.
  • 2026 national and regional shortage lists explicitly include broad IT/data-processing occupation groups.
  • Multiple skilled routes: EU Blue Card, shortage RWR, Other Key Worker and a job-seeker option for very highly qualified candidates.
  • Official international-recruitment infrastructure through WORK in AUSTRIA.
  • Strong industrial, engineering and research ecosystems alongside core ICT.
  • Clear 12-month post-study and post-research job-search options.

Things to consider

  • ICT job-ad volumes fell in 2025 in several roles, so the market is selective despite structural shortages.
  • German can materially affect access outside international teams.
  • A shortage-list entry does not automatically make every ICT job eligible; occupation, training, job offer and points must align.
  • The Blue Card requires the annual threshold and an AMS labour-market test.
  • Austria is a smaller absolute labour market than Germany; direct international recruitment is concentrated among suitable employers.
  • Housing and living costs in major hubs should be assessed against net pay and relocation support.

India–Austria mobility corridor

01Demand, role families and verified benchmarks

Where are the ICT opportunities?

Austria has a diversified technology market embedded across software and IT services, manufacturing, engineering, finance, telecommunications, energy and research. ICT specialists represented 5.4% of total employment in 2025, above the EU average of 5.0%. The European Commission nevertheless identifies persistent ICT specialist shortages as a constraint on innovation and digital transformation.

AMS JobBarometer recorded 20,948 online advertisements for software developers in 2025, by far the largest selected ICT occupation, alongside substantial demand for IT project managers, cybersecurity specialists, systems/network roles, QA and data specialists. Volumes fell from the post-pandemic peak in several occupations, so the evidence points to strong structural need combined with more selective hiring rather than a blanket vacancy market.

Where are the jobs?

Main ICT hubs

  • Vienna: largest software, corporate IT, consulting, startup and research market; 7,413 software-developer ads in 2025.
  • Linz / Upper Austria: strong industrial, manufacturing, automation and enterprise-technology base; 3,795 software-developer ads.
  • Graz / Styria: software, engineering, mobility, electronics and research; 3,077 software-developer ads.
  • Salzburg, Lower Austria, Tyrol and Vorarlberg: smaller but meaningful regional ICT demand.

Employer landscape

  • One in five Austrian enterprises employed ICT specialists in the 2024 enterprise ICT survey; larger firms were more likely to do so.
  • ICT hiring spans core technology plus manufacturing, engineering, finance, telecom, energy and business services.
  • WORK in AUSTRIA, acting for the Federal Government, explicitly focuses international recruitment support on IT and other high-impact technical sectors.
  • For candidates in India, employer capacity for immigration, relocation and English-language onboarding should be checked vacancy by vacancy.

04What the verified Austrian evidence suggests

Applying from India

  • Target a defined specialist profile: Software development is the largest selected ICT occupation in the AMS JobBarometer; cybersecurity, project management, data and infrastructure also show material demand. The 2026 shortage lists cover many IT/data-processing occupations, but eligibility depends on the exact occupational classification and training.
  • Show current technical depth and impact: architecture/ownership, production scale, security and reliability, cloud/data tooling, stakeholder responsibility and measurable outcomes.
  • Check immigration fit and employer readiness early: The Blue Card has a 2026 salary threshold and labour-market test; RWR routes may require points, a shortage match or a separate labour-market test. Confirm relocation and third-country hiring support for each vacancy.
  • Treat German as a career multiplier: English can work in international technology and research teams, but German opens more SMEs and client-facing roles and can also add points under relevant RWR categories.
  • Prepare documents early: passport, degree/transcripts, detailed employment references, language evidence used for points, police-clearance and civil-status documents. The Austrian Embassy New Delhi says English supporting documents generally need no German translation unless requested; legalisation requirements remain case-specific.

Evidence basis: AMS/Statistics Austria, 2026 shortage lists, Austrian migration platform and WORK in AUSTRIA. No Austria-specific EULGO employer or academic mapping was available; no employer statements are attributed to EULGO.

05

Recruitment: know your rights

India application point

Residence permits for stays over six months, work-purpose visas and the Job-Seeker Visa are applied for in person at the Austrian Embassy New Delhi by applicants resident in its consular area. Appointments for these categories are arranged with the Embassy. English-language documents normally do not require German translation unless requested; legalisation and route-specific document requirements must still be checked.

Before accepting an offer

  • Verify the Austrian legal entity, job title, location, working time, gross salary and contract duration.
  • Confirm the applicable residence route, salary threshold, points and shortage classification before relying on the offer.
  • Check the applicable collective agreement and the minimum remuneration shown in the job advertisement.
  • Confirm who handles the employer declaration, AMS process, relocation and residence steps.
  • Keep written copies of the offer, contract and immigration communications.

Red flags

  • A recruiter guarantees approval of an RWR Card, EU Blue Card or Job-Seeker Visa.
  • Payment is requested for a non-verifiable vacancy, permit or “guaranteed” job offer.
  • The employer, salary, work location or role differs between the recruitment message and official documents.
  • You are told to start work before you have the required work/residence right.
  • An unsolicited message claims to be a job offer from ABA / WORK in AUSTRIA; ABA warns that it does not send unsolicited job offers.

Study conditions & career signals

  • Around 580 English-taught programmes are listed nationally; many are at master’s level.
  • Public-university tuition for third-country students with a Residence Permit-Student is generally EUR 751.92/semester, plus EUR 25.20 compulsory ÖH/accident fee.
  • Universities of applied sciences and private institutions set their own fees.
  • Working during studies: Up to 20 hours/week with an AMS employment permit requested by the employer; no labour-market test for this permit.
  • Eligible graduates can extend their student residence permit once for 12 months to seek work or start a business.
  • Scholarships and funding opportunities should be checked via grants.at/OeAD and the institution.

Illustrative institutions, not a ranking or complete ICT catalogue. Confirm programme language, admissions, tuition exemptions, scholarships and degree-specific requirements with the institution and Study in Austria / Studienwahl.

03

From study to employment

  1. Complete eligible higher education in Austria
  2. Extend student permit for 12-month job search
  3. Use career services and permitted student work
  4. Secure a qualifying graduate ICT job
  5. Switch to graduate RWR / Blue Card / other eligible status

Important post-study advantage

Eligible graduates may extend their Residence Permit - Student once for 12 months to seek employment or establish a business, subject to the ordinary residence requirements. This extension does not grant unrestricted work rights: employment of up to 20 hours a week may be authorised by an AMS permit requested by the employer, without a labour-market test. A graduate job of at least 30 hours/week corresponding to the qualification and paying the locally customary graduate rate may qualify for a Red-White-Red Card for graduates without a labour-market check, subject to the remaining conditions.

Research opportunities

Austria has strong research activity in computer science, AI/data, cybersecurity, quantum technologies, microelectronics, robotics and industrial digitalisation. Major actors include TU Wien, TU Graz, JKU, University of Vienna, Innsbruck, Klagenfurt, ISTA, AIT and Silicon Austria Labs. Use EURAXESS Austria and institutional career pages for live calls.

Find opportunities

  • EURAXESS Austria / EURAXESS Europe.
  • TU Wien, TU Graz, JKU Linz, University of Vienna, Innsbruck and Klagenfurt career/doctoral pages.
  • Institute of Science and Technology Austria (ISTA).
  • Austrian Institute of Technology (AIT).
  • Silicon Austria Labs and other applied R&D organisations.
  • Research project and doctoral calls should be checked directly with the host.

For researchers & doctoral candidates

  • The Settlement Permit – Researcher requires a doctoral/PhD or degree enabling doctoral study and a hosting agreement with an eligible research institution.
  • It is normally issued for two years, or hosting-agreement duration + three months if shorter.
  • The research activity in the hosting agreement needs no additional work permit.
  • Family members can receive RWR Card Plus with free labour-market access.
  • After research is completed, the permit can be extended for 12 months to seek work or set up a business.

Frequently asked questions

Recognition of Indian qualifications, working in English and moving with your family in Austria.

For ICT professionals

  • Most software, data, cloud, cybersecurity and product roles are non-regulated.
  • Formal recognition is usually not needed to practise them.
  • Immigration authorities still assess route-specific qualification/training/experience evidence.
  • The Blue Card can accept qualifying ICT experience instead of a degree in defined cases; the shortage route requires matching completed training.

Austrian recognition system

  • Formal recognition is required only for regulated professions.
  • Berufsanerkennung.at identifies regulated occupations and responsible authorities.
  • For non-regulated ICT jobs, RWR/Blue Card qualification evidence is assessed within the immigration procedure.
  • Recognition itself does not create a residence or work right.

English works in international ICT—but German broadens the market

English can be sufficient in multinational technology and research teams. German materially widens access to SMEs and local/client-facing roles. The EU Blue Card has no points-based language requirement; relevant RWR categories award points for German and/or English.

Family members can obtain unrestricted labour-market access through the RWR Card Plus

Eligible spouses/registered partners and unmarried minor children of Red-White-Red Card or EU Blue Card holders can apply for a Red-White-Red Card Plus, which provides free access to the labour market. Family members of RWR, EU Blue Card and researcher permit holders are not subject to the family quota. A1 German is normally required for many first family applications, but family members of EU Blue Card holders, RWR Very Highly Qualified Workers and Settlement Permit – Researcher holders are exempt from pre-entry German proof. General income, insurance, housing and documentary requirements still apply.
