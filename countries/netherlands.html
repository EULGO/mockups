In brief

The Netherlands is a large, ICT-intensive labour market: ICT specialists were 7.2% of employment in 2025 and about 560,000 people worked in ICT occupations. Recruitment tightness has eased from its peak, but experienced specialists still have credible opportunities in hubs such as Amsterdam / Randstad and Eindhoven / Brainport, mainly through the Highly Skilled Migrant and EU Blue Card routes.

03Balanced candidate assessment

Why consider the Netherlands?

Potential advantages

  • Large ICT workforce and 7.2% ICT-specialist share of employment in 2025.
  • Strong software, fintech, cloud, semiconductor/high-tech, data/AI, cybersecurity and research ecosystems.
  • High enterprise digitalisation and AI adoption.
  • Established HSM and EU Blue Card routes, including an experience pathway for qualifying ICT Blue Card applicants.
  • English is widely used in many international technology and R&D environments.
  • Established India-Netherlands knowledge-migration and research corridor.

Things to consider

  • ICT hiring has cooled from its tightest period; a specialist vacancy and relevant experience matter more than a generic shortage label.
  • HSM salary thresholds are high and HSM sponsorship is limited to recognised employers.
  • Dutch can be important for locally oriented, client-facing and public-sector roles.
  • Housing availability and costs in the Randstad and Eindhoven should be assessed early against net salary and relocation support.
  • Immigration thresholds and procedural rules change and must be rechecked at application.
  • Partner work rights are more restrictive under GVVA and student-family situations than under HSM/Blue Card/research routes.

01Demand, role families and verified benchmarks

Where are the ICT opportunities?

The Netherlands has a large, internationally connected digital and high-tech economy spanning software and cloud services, fintech, data/AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, embedded systems, telecommunications, logistics technology and research. ICT specialists represented 7.2% of employment in 2025, and UWV reported around 560,000 people working in ICT occupations. Eurostat also recorded AI use by 33.2% of Dutch enterprises in 2025, underlining rapid technology adoption beyond the core ICT sector.

Current official evidence points to a more selective market than during the tightest post-pandemic years: ICT shortages remain relevant, but restructuring, weaker vacancy growth and productivity gains have reduced blanket recruitment pressure. Experienced software, data/AI, cloud, cybersecurity and high-tech engineering profiles remain the most credible starting points. 02

From job offer to arrival

  1. Target a specialist role and check sponsor/status
  2. Confirm route, salary and qualification/experience
  3. Employer submits the IND / UWV process
  4. Legalise/translate documents and await decision
  5. Collect the MVV sticker in India if required
  6. Travel, collect residence card and register / obtain BSN

The employer usually leads the immigration filing for work-based routes. IND decision periods differ: paid-employment applications can have a 90-day statutory period, researcher applications 60 days, and EU Blue Card cases 30, 45 or 90 days depending on the circumstances. Recognised sponsors can benefit from faster target processing for complete applications. After a positive IND decision, applicants whose decision letter directs them to India can book an appointment for the MVV sticker in New Delhi, Bangalore or Mumbai; NetherlandsWorldwide states that the sticker should be requested within three months of the positive decision and may take up to 10 working days.

04What verified Netherlands evidence suggests

Applying from India

  • Target a defined specialist profile rather than a broad “ICT professional” label. The strongest candidate propositions are specific - for example backend/platform engineering, cloud/SRE, ML engineering, cybersecurity, semiconductor software or embedded systems - and should be matched to a live employer need.
  • Demonstrate current production experience and the level at which you operate. UWV evidence indicates that ICT labour-market tightness has eased and that experience remains important, so applications should show ownership, scale, reliability, security, measurable impact and the exact technologies used rather than rely on a general shortage narrative.
  • Assess immigration readiness as carefully as technical fit. For the Highly Skilled Migrant route, the employer must be an IND-recognised sponsor and the salary must meet the applicable 2026 criterion. A Blue Card can be used without recognised-sponsor status, but it has its own salary and qualification/experience rules. Confirm route, filing responsibility and relocation support before investing heavily in the process.
  • Make language and integration readiness visible. English is a genuine working language in many international technology and high-tech environments, but Dutch can broaden access to local clients, consulting, public-sector roles and longer-term career options.
  • Prepare documents early. Keep your passport, degree, transcripts, detailed employment references and civil-status documents ready. IND applications can require foreign public documents to be legalised and translated into Dutch, English, French or German. After a positive decision, follow the NetherlandsWorldwide instructions for the MVV sticker location and appointment in India.

05

Recruitment: know your rights

India application point

After a positive IND decision, applicants whose decision letter states that the MVV can be collected in India can make an appointment at the Dutch mission in New Delhi, Bangalore or Mumbai. NetherlandsWorldwide advises requesting the sticker within three months of the positive decision; processing can take up to 10 working days.

Before accepting an offer

  • Verify the employer legal entity and, for the HSM route, confirm it in the current IND Public Register of Recognised Sponsors.
  • Check the gross base salary against the current immigration criterion and whether the salary is market-rate for the role.
  • Confirm working hours, contract duration, holiday allowance, probation, notice, pension/benefits and relocation/housing support in writing.
  • Confirm which route will be filed, who pays immigration costs and when you may legally start work.
  • Check partner and family work rights because they differ between HSM/Blue Card/research, GVVA and study routes.

Red flags

  • A recruiter guarantees approval of an MVV or residence permit.
  • Money is requested for a non-verifiable job, invitation, sponsorship or “guaranteed” recognised-sponsor placement.
  • An employer claims to use the HSM route but cannot be verified in the IND sponsor register.
  • Salary, role, employer or work location differs between the offer and immigration documents.
  • You are told to begin work before the required work/residence authorisation is valid.
  • Recognised-sponsor status is presented as proof that a vacancy or intermediary is legitimate - verify the vacancy separately.

02

Higher education options & costs

Selected ICT-relevant institutions

  • TU Delft: major engineering and computer-science research environment, with strong links to deep tech and high-tech industry.
  • Eindhoven University of Technology: strong fit for computer science, AI, data, embedded and semiconductor/high-tech ecosystems.
  • University of Twente: computer science, cybersecurity, embedded systems, data and deep-tech strengths.
  • University of Amsterdam and Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam: broad computer science, AI and data-science ecosystems.
  • Radboud University and University of Groningen: additional internationally oriented computing/data/research options.
  • AcademicTransfer is a useful cross-institution channel for doctoral and research vacancies.

Study conditions & career signals

  • Study in NL gives average non-EU/EEA tuition of about EUR 9,000-20,000/year for bachelor programmes and EUR 12,000-30,000/year for master programmes; exact fees vary by institution/programme.
  • The educational institution must be an IND-recognised sponsor for the student residence permit.
  • Working during studies: An employer must obtain a TWV; paid employment is limited to 16 hours/week or full-time in June-August. Self-employment is subject to different rules.
  • The student permit normally covers the study period plus three months, up to five years.
  • Eligible Dutch graduates, doctoral candidates and researchers can use the orientation year to bridge into skilled employment; current route criteria apply when transitioning.

The institution list is illustrative rather than a ranking or complete programme catalogue. Programme language, admission requirements, tuition, scholarships and intake availability must be confirmed directly with the institution and Study in NL. Immigration work rights must be checked with IND.

Orientation year can bridge Dutch study/research to skilled employment

Eligible graduates, doctoral candidates and researchers may apply to the IND for a one-year orientation-year residence permit, generally within three years of qualifying completion; some eligible foreign degrees also qualify. Holders can work freely without an employer TWV throughout the orientation year. Before it expires, applicants wishing to remain should change to the Highly Skilled Migrant, EU Blue Card or other suitable residence basis; the relevant eligibility and salary rules apply.The IND orientation-year permit can be requested within three years of qualifying Dutch graduation, doctorate or research and in certain cases after qualifying foreign study; the permit itself is valid for one year. During the orientation year, the holder can work freely without a TWV. Candidates who meet the statutory conditions can later benefit from the reduced Highly Skilled Migrant salary criterion (EUR 3,122/month in 2026). Family and work rights change when moving to a subsequent residence status.

Research opportunities

The Dutch research ecosystem is highly relevant for computer science, AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, photonics, robotics, embedded systems, quantum technology, digital health and data-intensive research. Nuffic counted 15,200 non-EEA researchers in 2025 and ranked India second among countries of origin; TU Delft was the largest institutional employer in the cited Nuffic overview. AcademicTransfer and EURAXESS list current research and doctoral opportunities, while universities, TNO and company R&D centres recruit directly.

Find opportunities

  • AcademicTransfer: central Dutch portal for academic and research vacancies.
  • EURAXESS Netherlands / Europe: research, doctoral and mobility opportunities.
  • TU Delft, Eindhoven University of Technology and University of Twente for engineering/high-tech research.
  • UvA, VU Amsterdam, Radboud and Groningen for AI, computing and data-intensive research.
  • TNO and company R&D centres in semiconductors, high-tech, software and applied AI.

For researchers & doctoral candidates

  • The EU researcher route normally requires a recognised research sponsor plus a hosting/employment agreement and suitable academic level.
  • It covers paid researchers, grant-funded researchers and many PhD positions, depending on the arrangement.
  • The research work can be performed without a TWV under the researcher permit.
  • Permits can generally be issued for up to five years and include specific short- and long-term EU research mobility provisions.
  • Project fit, supervisor/group match, funding and publication/technical evidence matter more than broad institutional promotion.

Frequently asked questions

Recognition of Indian qualifications, working in English and moving with your family in the Netherlands.

For ICT professionals

  • Most software, data, cloud, cybersecurity, product and high-tech ICT roles are non-regulated occupations; there is no general professional licence for practising them.
  • A credential evaluation is therefore usually not mandatory for employment itself, although an employer can request one.
  • Immigration routes can still require proof of higher education or qualifying professional experience.
  • For the EU Blue Card degree route, a foreign diploma is evaluated by Nuffic; qualifying ICT managers/professionals can alternatively rely on the specified experience route.
  • Keep degree certificates, transcripts and detailed employer references showing dates, duties, seniority and technologies.

Idw / nuffic credential evaluation

  • IDW/Nuffic compare foreign qualifications with Dutch education levels. The evaluation is advisory and does not itself grant admission, a job or a residence right.
  • Nuffic generally compares an Indian BCA with three years of higher professional education (HBO).
  • An Indian BE/BTech is generally comparable to an HBO bachelor; in qualifying cases from top institutions with strong results it may compare to an HBO or WO bachelor.
  • The employer, university and IND route still apply their own requirements; do not treat a generic comparison as automatic eligibility.
  • Document language, authenticity and legalisation requirements should be checked for the specific purpose.

Family work rights depend on the principal permit

Eligible partners of Highly Skilled Migrants, EU Blue Card holders, intra-corporate transferees and researchers are generally free to work in the Netherlands once the relevant family residence right is issued. A partner joining a student is not allowed to work, while a partner of a GVVA holder generally needs an employer to obtain a TWV. Family eligibility, income, documentary and residence conditions depend on the principal permit and must be checked with IND.
