Software/product engineering; data and AI/ML; cloud/DevOps/SRE; cybersecurity; solution architecture and ICT delivery; embedded/systems software.

EU Blue Card threshold

EUR 59,373 gross/year; contract of at least 6 months; qualification/experience conditions apply.

Digital-sector pay reference

2025 Numeum survey: EUR 45,540 median annual base salary; EUR 55,283 average annual total gross remuneration. These are sector benchmarks, not visa thresholds.

Student-to-work route

Eligible graduates/researchers can use the 1-year “recherche d’emploi / création d’entreprise” (RECE) status and transition to a work status when conditions are met.

Market signal: scale and specialisation matter more than a blanket “ICT shortage” label

France combines the EU’s second-largest ICT talent pool with renewed digital-market growth in 2026. At the same time, Numeum reports a more cautious recruitment environment than in 2023. Indian candidates are therefore best positioned when they target a verified vacancy, demonstrate mid-level or senior production experience, and match both the technical stack and the employer’s language and mobility requirements.

01Demand, role families and verified market signals

Where are the ICT opportunities?

France’s ICT opportunity is broad because digital skills are employed both inside core technology companies and across banking, aerospace, automotive, energy, retail, life sciences, telecommunications and public-sector digitalisation. Eurostat places France at about 1.4 million ICT specialists in 2025, while Numeum’s narrower digital-sector universe reports 666,000 employees.

The internal 2026 EULGO mapping covers 104 unique employers: 63 are classified as core ICT companies and 41 as ICT-intensive users. Seniority is weighted toward experienced talent (68 mid-level and 36 senior employer records). The mapping should be treated as a structured market signal, not a statistically representative vacancy survey.

01Which pathway could apply?

Moving to France for work

France has several employment routes relevant to Indian ICT talent. The correct route depends on salary, qualifications or experience, the employer and the candidate’s circumstances. The “Talent – salarié qualifié” route is especially important to interpret correctly: its standard salaried pathway requires an eligible higher-education qualification obtained in France, so an Indian degree alone does not automatically qualify a candidate for that specific route.

02

From job offer to arrival

StepWhat happensCandidate check

1. Secure a verified offer

Employer confirms role, contract, salary, location and intended immigration route.

Check whether salary and qualifications match the intended route.

2. Confirm employer process

For Blue Card/Talent routes, a separate work authorisation is generally not required. For standard salarié/travailleur temporaire, the employer normally applies online for work authorisation before entry.

Ask the employer who owns the immigration process and whether they have recruited non-EU staff before.

3. Prepare evidence

Passport, contract, qualifications/experience evidence and route-specific documents.

Prepare translations/recognition evidence only where actually required; do not buy unnecessary “verification” services.

4. Apply for long-stay visa

From abroad, application is made through the French visa process/consular authorities; India uses the France-Visas/Campus France architecture as relevant to the purpose of stay.

Use only official portals and authorised service providers.

5. Complete post-arrival formalities

Depending on visa/permit, validate the VLS-TS or apply online for the residence card through ANEF within the applicable time limit.

Keep proof of address and digital copies of immigration documents.

6. Start and maintain status

Work under the authorised conditions; renew/change status before expiry when needed.

Do not assume a permit issued for one status automatically covers a new employer/role or another EU Member State.

04What the mapping suggests

Applying from India

  • Target a defined role family and stack: “ICT professional” is too broad for a selective French market; specify, for example, Java backend, cloud platform/SRE, SAP, cybersecurity, embedded C/C++, data engineering or MLOps.
  • Lead with recent delivery evidence: The France mapping is entirely mid-level/senior in its employer seniority field (68 mid-level; 36 senior), so demonstrate ownership, production scope, complexity and measurable outcomes.
  • Make language readiness explicit: Where French is not yet strong, state the level, active learning plan and whether you have worked in multilingual/global teams.
  • Check immigration viability before late-stage interviews: A technically suitable offer below the Blue Card threshold may need another route and potentially employer work authorisation.
  • Assess employer readiness: Ask whether the company has a French entity, has sponsored third-country nationals before, and supports visa/relocation processes. A famous brand does not guarantee mobility support for every role.
  • Prepare for skills-based assessment: for experienced ICT roles, portfolios, GitHub/code samples where appropriate, architecture examples, cloud/security certifications and concise project case studies can complement formal degrees.

05

Recruitment: know your rights

DoAvoid / red flag

Verify the employer, vacancy, salary and contractual entity before sending sensitive documents.

Promises of a “guaranteed French work visa” without a genuine employer and eligible route.

Use France-Visas, Service-Public/ANEF and official employer documentation for immigration steps.

Paying large recruitment or “visa reservation” fees to unverified intermediaries.

Ensure salary, working time, location and contract type are written and consistent with the immigration route.

A salary shown only after “deductions” or a route that depends on a threshold the gross contract does not meet.

Ask who pays relocation/immigration costs and what happens if the permit is delayed or refused.

Pressure to resign or pay deposits before the employer has completed its part of the process.

Check any recruitment intermediary independently.

Sending original diplomas/passports to an unknown agent or accepting unofficial document-verification claims.

From study to employment

A strong pathway

Eligible graduates can obtain the one-year, non-renewable recherche d’emploi / création d’entreprise (RECE) status. They may work without separate employer authorisation where the job relates to their studies/research and pays more than EUR 2,800.53 gross/month (2026 guidance). To remain afterwards, they must qualify for an appropriate professional residence title.

Stage

Key rule

ICT candidate implication

1. Complete eligible studies / research

Eligibility for RECE depends on the qualifying French study/research status and credential/research completion.

Confirm eligibility with the institution and Service-Public before relying on the route.

2. RECE – 1 year

Allows job search / business creation and salaried work; employer does not need a work authorisation during RECE validity.

Use the period to obtain role-relevant French experience and secure a qualifying offer.

3. Standard salarié route after RECE

Employment related to the training/research with remuneration of at least EUR 2,800.53 gross/month can qualify for the simplified transition indicated by Service-Public; labour-market testing is not applied in this case.

Important option where salary is below Talent/Blue Card thresholds.

4. Talent – salarié qualifié

From EUR 39,582 gross/year, subject to the route’s other conditions, including the qualifying French degree.

Relevant to eligible French Master/CGE graduates.

5. EU Blue Card

From EUR 59,373 gross/year plus qualification/experience and contract requirements.

Most relevant to high-paying specialist/senior offers.

Frequently asked questions

Recognition of Indian qualifications, working in English and moving with your family in France.

For most candidate-facing ICT recruitment, distinguish professional recognition from immigration evidence

France Education International’s ENIC-NARIC centre can issue a non-binding attestation of comparability for a foreign academic qualification. It helps employers, institutions and administrations understand the level of a foreign diploma, but it is not mandatory in every case and does not itself grant the right to practise a regulated profession. For non-regulated ICT roles, employers generally assess suitability; the immigration route may still require specific qualification or experience evidence.

  • Check the vacancy first: determine whether the occupation itself is regulated and whether the employer asks for a comparability statement.
  • For the EU Blue Card, a 3-year higher-education qualification or qualifying professional experience can satisfy the high-qualification condition; documentary evidence is required.
  • For the standard “Talent – salarié qualifié” salaried pathway, the relevant Master-equivalent degree must be obtained in France; do not confuse this with general foreign-degree recognition.
  • Keep degree certificates, transcripts, detailed employment certificates and certified translations where requested.

However, French remains the default language in many ICT workplaces and becomes more important in consulting, project delivery, enterprise transformation, public-sector work and roles with local clients.

English can be the primary or a major working language in selected firms.

French frequently matters because of local client interaction and documentation.

B1/B2 French can materially expand opportunity, especially for analyst, architect, PM and consulting roles.

French and additional eligibility constraints may apply.

Verify the vacancy; do not rely on general “English-speaking tech” messaging.

Mapping of 92 ICT education/training records shows 45 national-language records, 22 bilingual records and 25 records explicitly including English.

Programme-level verification is essential; English availability is strongest in selected Master/doctoral/international tracks.

QuestionTalent-route positionCandidate note

Can spouse / children accompany?

Spouse and eligible minor children of a Talent permit holder can use the simplified “talent-famille” accompanying-family route rather than the standard family-reunification procedure.

Confirm family documentation and visa timing together with the principal applicant.

Can the spouse work?

Yes. The “talent-famille” residence card authorises salaried and self-employed professional activity; a salaried employer does not need a separate work authorisation.

A significant advantage for experienced professionals relocating with a partner.

Does this apply to every work route?

No. Family rules differ for standard salarié/travailleur temporaire and other statuses.

Verify family rights for the exact permit, not simply “working in France”.
