In brief

Germany employed 2.3 million ICT specialists in 2025 (5.5% of employment), about 31,000 of them Indian, and shows strong structural demand for advanced digital skills. Its hubs range from Berlin, Munich and Hamburg to Stuttgart and Frankfurt / Rhine-Main, and the main routes are the EU Blue Card, the qualified-professional visas and the Opportunity Card for job search.

02

ICT mobility at a glance

Market signal: opportunity, not a blanket shortage

Germany remains one of Europe's deepest ICT labour markets, but the 2025 slowdown matters. BA-registered ICT vacancies fell 22% to around 13,000 and ICT unemployment rose to 4.5%. At the same time, social-insurance ICT employment grew 2% to 1.15 million; more than 40% of 39,000 newly registered ICT jobs targeted experts and about one third targeted specialists. Software development was no longer classified as a BA bottleneck occupation in 2025, although specialist-level shortages and longer-term structural demand remain.

03Balanced candidate assessment

Why consider Germany?

Potential advantages

  • Europe's largest absolute ICT specialist workforce: 2.3 million in 2025.
  • Deep demand across technology and ICT-intensive industries, not only pure software firms.
  • Multiple legal routes for qualified and experienced IT professionals, including options without a formal degree.
  • Established India-Germany talent corridor: around 31,000 Indian ICT specialists already employed in Germany.
  • Large higher-education and research ecosystem, with India already the largest international student origin country.
  • Clear post-study transition route and strong pathways from study/research into employment.

Things to consider

  • The ICT hiring market softened in 2025; junior/generalist roles can be competitive.
  • German language requirements vary substantially and can exclude English-only candidates from parts of the market.
  • Visa, local administration and relocation logistics can create friction for both employers and hires.
  • Housing is a recurring relocation challenge in major hubs and was raised in employer interviews.
  • Some companies are increasing nearshore/offshore delivery in other European markets and India, so not every ICT need translates into relocation hiring.

01Demand, role families and salary benchmarks

Where are the ICT opportunities?

Germany combines a very large digital employer base with strong ICT demand embedded across the wider economy - from software and consulting to automotive, industrial technology, finance, logistics, retail and energy. The latest Bitkom forecast expects the German IT and telecommunications market to reach EUR 246.4 billion in 2026. Cloud, software, AI and cybersecurity remain important growth and investment themes.

EULGO's employer scan of 118 major companies in May 2026 repeatedly identified senior software engineering, AI/data, cloud/platform, cybersecurity, enterprise architecture/SAP and engineering-intensive software profiles. These findings are used as a directional market signal, not as an estimate of total vacancies.

02

Where are the jobs?

Main ICT hubs

  • Berlin: start-ups/scale-ups, software, fintech, platforms and international English-speaking teams.
  • Munich: large tech, enterprise software, automotive/mobility, AI, industrial digitalisation and deep tech.
  • Stuttgart & Southern Germany: automotive, engineering, embedded systems, industrial software and enterprise technology.
  • Frankfurt / Rhine-Main: finance, cloud/data infrastructure, consulting and enterprise IT.
  • Hamburg: digital services, logistics, media, e-commerce and enterprise technology.
  • Cologne-Duesseldorf / Rhine-Ruhr: telecoms, consulting, corporate IT, retail and industry.
  • Darmstadt / Rhine-Neckar, Nuremberg & Dresden: research, enterprise technology, semiconductors, industrial and specialist ICT ecosystems.

Employer landscape

  • Large multinationals and German industrial groups recruit significant ICT talent in addition to core technology companies.
  • Germany also has a broad Mittelstand/SME base; international hiring capacity and English-language working environments vary considerably.
  • EULGO's 118-company scan included multinationals, large private/public companies and fast-growing scale-ups, illustrating that ICT talent demand is distributed far beyond the classic software sector.
  • Some employers are simultaneously expanding nearshore/offshore capacity in lower-cost European locations and in India, so international mobility opportunities should be targeted toward roles where on-site presence, scarce expertise or team integration justify relocation.

Moving to Germany for work

Germany has several routes that are particularly relevant to ICT talent. The best route depends on your qualification, experience, salary, age and whether you already have a concrete job offer.   4. Submit national visa application online

Processing time varies by route, case and mission, so candidates should avoid relying on a fixed timeline.

Finding an ICT job from India

Official / high-value channels

  • Make it in Germany job listings - vacancies aimed at international skilled workers.
  • Federal Employment Agency Jobsuche - Germany's largest official job search service.
  • EURES - EU/EEA mobility and vacancy information.
  • EURAXESS - research positions, funding and researcher mobility.
  • Employer career pages - especially important for large industrial and technology companies.

What the employer mapping shows

  • LinkedIn was the only recruitment channel mentioned across every completed employer/recruiter interview and functions as an industry default for sourcing and employer branding.
  • Stepstone, specialised recruiters, employee referrals, targeted advertising and tech events are commonly complementary channels.
  • For senior or specialist roles, employers often look beyond job titles to evidence of tasks, impact, product context and recent experience.
  • International recruitment is more likely to be justified when the candidate brings scarce expertise or when local/European sourcing has not produced a strong match.

04Employer reality from the 2026 stakeholder mapping

Applying from India

Target specialised roles, not only high-volume generalist vacancies. The German market is large, but 2025 was weaker and employers reported higher selectivity. Expert/specialist roles are the strongest entry point for direct international hiring.

Tailor the application to the role and the company. Interviews flagged a growing burden from generic and AI-generated applications. Show knowledge of the product/business and link your experience to the actual technical problem.

Demonstrate ownership and cross-cultural readiness. Some employers specifically value candidates who can work in flatter structures, take ownership and communicate across distributed teams. Prior international study/work experience can help signal readiness.

Make relocation readiness visible. Visa complexity, housing, insurance, childcare and onboarding effort were recurrent employer concerns. Candidates who understand the route, timing and location requirements reduce perceived hiring risk.

Treat German as a career multiplier. English may be sufficient for some international technology teams, but even basic-to-intermediate German can materially widen the employer pool and support integration.

Qualitative evidence: EULGO Germany interviews with employers/recruiters in June 2026. Findings are directional and should not be interpreted as representative survey statistics.

05

Recruitment: know your rights

Before accepting an offer

  • Verify the employer, role, location, salary and working conditions in the written contract.
  • Confirm which residence route applies and whether your salary meets any applicable threshold.
  • Use official German government sources for visa and recognition requirements.
  • Check recruiters/intermediaries carefully and keep copies of contracts, correspondence and receipts.
  • Ensure that the job is genuinely qualified employment where the residence route requires it.

Red flags

  • A guaranteed German visa or EU Blue Card in exchange for payment.
  • Large recruitment fees for access to a vacancy or a promised permit.
  • Pressure to provide sensitive documents before the employer or vacancy can be verified.
  • A contract whose salary, role or location differs from what was presented during recruitment.
  • Claims that one Member State residence title automatically allows long-term work in every other EU country.

06

Practical information

Before you move

  • Compare salary against the actual city and role - German ICT salaries vary significantly by region and seniority.
  • Budget for temporary accommodation and a potentially difficult housing search in major hubs.
  • Confirm health-insurance arrangements and the employer's onboarding support before travel.
  • Keep original qualification, employment and civil-status documents available for visa/residence procedures.
  • Ask whether the employer supports visa acceleration, relocation, registration, housing or family onboarding.

On arrival

  • Complete local registration (Anmeldung) and residence steps required for your status.
  • Ensure payroll, health insurance, tax and bank arrangements are in place.
  • Use employer/integration support where available; relocation capacity varies greatly across companies.
  • Continue German learning even in an English-speaking role - it supports wider career mobility and settlement pathways.

Higher education options & costs

Selected ICT-relevant institutions

  • Technical University of Munich (TUM)
  • RWTH Aachen University
  • TU Berlin and HTW Berlin
  • Saarland University
  • FAU Erlangen-Nuernberg
  • TU Dresden
  • Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt
  • University of Bonn and IU Internationale Hochschule

Study practicalities

  • Many public universities charge no general tuition for bachelor's and most master's programmes, but semester contributions apply.
  • Important exceptions exist: Baden-Wuerttemberg charges many non-EU students EUR 1,500 per semester; fees can also apply at particular universities/programmes, including in Bavaria.
  • Programme language requirements vary; study visa guidance notes that proof is often around B2 where required by the programme.
  • Working during studies: Up to 140 full days or 280 half days per year, or up to 20 hours/week under the applicable counting rules; student ancillary jobs have separate rules.

Institution list reflects the EULGO Germany academic stakeholder mapping and is not a ranking. Programme language, tuition and admissions must be checked with the institution and DAAD/Higher Education Compass.

03

From study to employment

  1. Complete German degree
  2. Apply for post-study job-search residence
  3. Up to 18 months to find qualified employment
  4. Work without restriction while searching
  5. Switch to Blue Card / skilled-worker title once qualified job is secured

Why this matters for Indian ICT talent

Eligible graduates of a German higher-education institution may apply for an 18-month post-study residence permit to find a qualified job. They can work without restriction during this search period. Apply to the local foreigners authority while the current study permit remains valid; once a qualifying offer is secured, apply for the relevant skilled-worker or EU Blue Card title. This pathway also gives employers access to candidates with German qualifications and local experience; DAAD research reports high stay intentions in engineering and computer science.

04

Research opportunities

Germany is a major European research destination with strong ecosystems in AI, cybersecurity, quantum technologies, semiconductors, robotics, industrial digitalisation and advanced engineering. For a research residence route, candidates generally need a hosting agreement or work contract with a research institution and an academic qualification that gives access to doctoral study. After completing research, a specific residence route can allow up to 18 months to seek qualified employment.

Find opportunities

  • EURAXESS Germany / EURAXESS Europe
  • University and research-institute career portals
  • DAAD databases and scholarship information
  • Max Planck, Fraunhofer, Helmholtz and Leibniz institutes, where relevant to the field

For doctoral candidates

  • If employed under a research contract/hosting arrangement, the research residence route can apply.
  • If enrolled primarily as a full-time doctoral student without the research employment arrangement, the study residence route may be the relevant route.
  • Always confirm the correct residence basis with the host institution and official immigration guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Recognition of Indian qualifications, working in English and moving with your family in Germany.

For ICT professionals

  • Computer science / IT occupations are generally non-regulated in Germany: formal professional recognition is normally not required simply to practise the occupation.
  • Immigration rules are separate. The chosen residence title may require proof that a foreign degree is comparable or a qualification is recognised.
  • For academic degrees, anabin can provide evidence of comparability; if the database does not provide sufficient proof, a ZAB Statement of Comparability may be used.
  • Some IT routes explicitly allow work experience to substitute for a formal degree, which is particularly relevant for experienced technology professionals.

What employers told EULGO

  • Skills-based hiring is becoming more important in technology recruitment, and recent work evidence can carry more weight than degree pedigree.
  • However, immigration/qualification rules and company hiring practices are not the same thing: a company may be skills-first while a specific visa still requires formal evidence.
  • For candidates, a clear portfolio of recent technical impact, role scope, architecture responsibility and certifications can make experience easier to assess.

English is possible - but German changes the opportunity set

There is no general German-language requirement for the EU Blue Card, work visa for qualified professionals or research residence route. In the labour market, however, good German significantly broadens access. EULGO interviews found English-first environments in international product/tech teams, while German was more important in traditional SMEs, in-person cultures and legal, finance, client-facing or locally embedded roles. For the Opportunity Card points route, at least German A1 or English B2 is required.

Family mobility is possible for skilled workers

Skilled workers and EU Blue Card holders can generally bring spouses and minor children through family reunification, subject to the applicable conditions. For spouses joining a skilled worker with a valid German residence title, prior proof of German is not required; the joining spouse is allowed to work in Germany. Check the specific requirements for your residence title, insurance and family situation before applying.
