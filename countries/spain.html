In brief

Spain had more than 1 million ICT specialists in 2025 (4.8% of employment) and strong demand for selected digital profiles in its major hubs, although it is not a blanket shortage market. Madrid and Barcelona lead, followed by Valencia, Málaga and Seville, and the main routes are the EU Blue Card, the national highly qualified professional permit and the general employed-worker permit.

02

ICT mobility at a glance

Market signal: growth, but not a universal shortage

Official INE data show 82,323 ICT-sector companies in 2024 (+7.2%), 688,727 people employed in the sector (+5.1%) and EUR 143.3 billion in turnover (+4.8%). Eurostat counted just over one million ICT specialists across the whole economy in 2025. At the same time, Spain’s total unemployment rate was 10.5% in 2025. The realistic opportunity is therefore strongest for targeted, experienced and hard-to-source profiles—not every general ICT applicant.

03Balanced candidate assessment

Why consider Spain?

Potential advantages

  • Just over one million ICT specialists and a broad digital employer ecosystem.
  • Official ICT-sector growth in companies, employment, turnover and value added in 2024.
  • Diverse opportunities across software, telecom, cloud, AI/data, cybersecurity, enterprise tech and research.
  • Fast-track Law 14/2013 routes and comparatively accessible 2026 Blue Card/HQP salary thresholds.
  • Large university/research system and a 24-month post-study search route.
  • Existing India-linked employers and verified study/work visa flows.

Things to consider

  • Spanish materially enlarges the market; English-only roles are concentrated.
  • Official salary levels are generally lower than in some northern and western EU destinations.
  • The employer mapping points mainly to mid-level and senior roles; junior/generalist applications can be competitive.
  • Madrid and Barcelona offer depth but require careful salary-versus-living-cost assessment.
  • The 24-month graduate search status does not itself authorise employment.

01Demand, role families and verified benchmarks

Where are the ICT opportunities?

Spain combines a large ICT workforce with a diverse digital economy spanning software and IT services, telecommunications, cloud and data infrastructure, enterprise technology, fintech, e-commerce, gaming, industrial digitalisation and research. Official INE data show continued growth in ICT-sector companies, employment and turnover in 2024.

The internal Spain mapping adds a demand-side view across 71 named employers, including both core ICT companies and ICT-intensive users. Its active-vacancy sample points most consistently to software/product engineering, cloud and DevOps, data/AI, cybersecurity, enterprise technology and specialised research or engineering roles. The mapping is directional and is not a statistically representative vacancy survey.

Where are the jobs?

Main ICT hubs

  • Madrid: enterprise technology, consulting, telecom, cloud, cybersecurity, public-sector digitalisation and multinational headquarters.
  • Barcelona: start-ups/scale-ups, product engineering, e-commerce, gaming, data/AI, supercomputing and international teams.
  • Valencia: software, cybersecurity, gaming, telecom and a growing start-up/innovation ecosystem.

Employer landscape

  • The internal mapping contains 71 named employers: 50 ICT-intensive users and 21 core ICT companies.

  • The sample supports role targeting and stakeholder engagement; it is not a census of all Spanish ICT employers.

01Which pathway could apply?

Moving to Spain for work

Spain offers several routes relevant to Indian ICT talent. The route depends on the role, salary, qualification or professional experience, whether the employer is using the fast-track mobility framework under Law 14/2013, and whether the candidate is being hired locally or transferred within a company.

PathwayBest suited forKey current conditionsLabour-market test / evidenceInitial perspective

EU Blue Card

Highly qualified ICT professionals, including experience-based ICT cases

Firm offer/contract of at least 6 months. Higher-education qualification or normally 5 years equivalent experience; ICT managers/professionals may qualify with 3 years relevant experience in the previous 7. 2026 salary: EUR 41,356.36; reduced EUR 33,085.09 in defined cases.

No national labour-market test. Qualification/experience and salary evidence required.

Fast-track UGE route; official decision period is 20 days. Verify current validity rules and reduced-threshold eligibility.

National highly qualified professional

Specialist or managerial roles meeting national high-qualification criteria

Higher qualification at least MECES level 1 / EQF 5A, or at least 3 years equivalent professional experience. 2026 salary threshold: EUR 41,356.36; no reduced national threshold.

No national labour-market test under Law 14/2013. Employer/role and candidate evidence assessed by UGE.

Useful where the national highly qualified route fits better than the Blue Card; official decision period is 20 days.

General employed-worker permit

Roles outside the strategic high-skilled routes

Employer applies with a signed contract for work longer than 90 days and shorter than 5 years. Conditions must comply with law and the applicable collective agreement.

National employment situation may need to permit recruitment unless the occupation/catalogue, recruitment test, agreement or statutory exception applies.

Broader route, but potentially more administratively demanding for recruitment directly from India.

Intra-company transfer (EU ICT / national)

Managers, specialists or trainees moving within a corporate group

Normally at least 3 months’ prior continuous group employment plus higher qualification or 3 years’ professional experience. EU ICT maximum: 3 years for managers/specialists; 1 year for trainees.

No general labour-market test; corporate relationship, assignment and qualification/experience must be documented.

Particularly relevant to Indian IT-service groups and multinationals with established Spanish entities.

Researcher residence

Researchers, technical staff, university teachers and R&D/innovation personnel

Contract or hosting arrangement with an eligible research/innovation entity; EU and national modalities. Permit normally up to 3 years or the contract duration if shorter.

No general labour-market test; research role, host entity and qualification conditions apply.

Fast-track route; official decision period 20 days. After research, up to 12 months may be available to seek work or start a business.

Primary sources: Spain’s Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration, the Large Companies and Strategic Groups Unit (UGE) and the current 2026 salary-threshold notice. Requirements can change and must be checked before application.

From job offer to arrival

  1. Find a suitable ICT role
  2. Confirm the correct Spanish route
  3. Prepare qualification / experience and civil documents
  4. Employer or UGE submits the authorisation
  5. Apply for national visa through the competent consular post
  6. Enter Spain; complete Social Security, local registration and TIE steps

For most work routes, the employer or authorised representative initiates the residence/work authorisation in Spain. After approval, an Indian national normally applies for the corresponding national visa through the competent Spanish consular post. Public documents from India may need an Apostille and a sworn Spanish translation. After arrival, registration, Social Security and the foreigner identity card (TIE), where applicable, must be completed.

04What the 2026 Spain mapping suggests

Applying from India

  • Target roles where international recruitment is most defensible. In the mapping, active priority roles were concentrated in experienced software engineering, cloud/DevOps, data and AI, cybersecurity, enterprise technology and specialised engineering profiles. Mid-level recruitment strongly outweighed junior recruitment.
  • Translate delivery experience into product and business impact. Indian candidates from IT services, consulting or global delivery environments should show ownership, architecture decisions, stakeholder communication, scale, reliability and measurable outcomes—not only technologies or project lists.
  • Make language readiness visible. English-only positions exist in multinational, start-up, research and globally distributed teams, especially in Madrid and Barcelona. Spanish substantially widens the market, while a co-official regional language may matter for some local or public-facing roles.
  • Prepare the mobility evidence early. Keep degree certificates, transcripts, detailed experience letters, job descriptions, police-clearance documents and civil-status documents organised. Check Apostille and sworn-translation requirements for the specific procedure before submission.
  • Tailor each application. Spain’s overall unemployment remains high and junior/generalist ICT roles can be competitive. A concise CV, role-specific portfolio, credible salary expectations and a clear willingness to relocate can help reduce employer uncertainty.

Qualitative evidence: EULGO Spain ICT Sector Mapping workbook, 2026. Employer-role findings are directional and should not be interpreted as representative survey statistics.

05

Recruitment: know your rights

Before accepting an offer

  • Verify the employer’s legal identity, role, work location, salary, working time, probation and contract conditions.
  • Confirm the exact residence route and the 2026 salary threshold where relevant; market salary and immigration threshold are different concepts.
  • Use official Spanish government and consular sources for immigration requirements.
  • Clarify who pays visa, translation, relocation and recruitment costs and what happens if the authorisation is refused.
  • Keep copies of the signed contract, offer, application receipt and all documents submitted.

Red flags

  • A guaranteed Spanish visa, Blue Card or job in exchange for payment.
  • Large recruitment fees or payment to obtain an interview or “sponsorship”.
  • A contract with salary, hours or employer information that differs from what was discussed.
  • Pressure to hand over original documents or sensitive data before the employer can be verified.
  • Claims that a post-study job-search permit lets you work immediately; it does not authorise work by itself.

Practical information

Before you move

  • Compare the gross offer with the city, seniority and expected net income; official sector averages are not individual offer guarantees.
  • Budget for initial accommodation and deposits, particularly in Madrid and Barcelona.
  • Confirm whether the role is English-first or requires Spanish from the start; begin language preparation early.
  • Complete Apostille and sworn-translation requirements before the visa appointment where required.
  • Clarify relocation support, start-date flexibility, family arrangements and remote/on-site expectations.

On arrival

  • Register locally (empadronamiento) where required for residence and public-service procedures.
  • Complete Social Security registration and obtain the TIE within the applicable deadline where the stay exceeds six months.
  • Arrange banking, payroll, tax identification and healthcare/social-security access.
  • Keep digital and physical copies of immigration decisions, visa, contract and registration documents.
  • Use employer, university, municipal and regional integration/language support where available.

Higher education options & costs

Selected ICT-relevant institutions

  • Universidad Politécnica de Madrid (UPM)
  • Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC)
  • Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (UC3M)
  • Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV)

  • University of Deusto; University of Seville and other mapped institutions

Study practicalities

  • Public undergraduate tuition is approximately EUR 700-EUR 1,800 per year; exact fees vary by autonomous community, credits and student status.
  • Private universities and some master’s/non-EU fee structures can be substantially higher.
  • Proof of funds is linked to 100% of IPREM; verify the amount and permitted deductions at the time of application.
  • Working during studies: Eligible higher-education study authorisation permits compatible employment or self-employment for up to 30 hours/week.
  • In the EULGO sample, most programmes were Spanish-language or bilingual; verify the actual teaching and assessment language.
  • Use official QEDU/RUCT and university portals to confirm programme recognition, admissions and fees.

The institution list reflects the internal Spain academic mapping and is not a ranking. Programme language, tuition, non-EU fees, admissions and scholarship conditions must be checked directly with the institution and official Spanish study portals.

03

From study to employment

  1. Complete eligible higher-education studies in Spain
  2. Apply for post-study search / entrepreneurship residence
  3. Use up to 24 non-renewable months to seek a suitable role
  4. Do not start work until a work-authorising status is obtained
  5. Switch to the appropriate Blue Card, HQP or other work route

Critical distinction for Indian graduates

Eligible higher-education graduates may apply for a non-renewable 24-month residence authorisation to seek employment appropriate to their studies or develop a business. The search authorisation does not itself permit employment: graduates must obtain a work-authorising status before starting a job. Apply under the applicable national procedure and transition to the EU Blue Card, highly qualified professional or other suitable work route once conditions are met.

Research opportunities

Spain has internationally relevant research ecosystems in high-performance computing, AI, quantum and advanced computing, cybersecurity, telecommunications, photonics, robotics, semiconductors and industrial digitalisation. Opportunities are concentrated in universities, public research bodies, technology centres and corporate R&D teams.

Find opportunities

  • Barcelona Supercomputing Center (BSC) and high-performance computing initiatives.
  • CSIC institutes and IMDEA research institutes.
  • i2CAT and other technology/research centres.
  • University research groups and doctoral programme portals.
  • Corporate R&D and innovation teams in telecom, mobility, semiconductors, cyber and industrial technology.

For researchers & doctoral candidates

  • The Law 14/2013 researcher route covers eligible researchers, scientific/technical staff, innovation personnel and university teachers.
  • An employment contract, hosting agreement or other eligible relationship with the host is required.
  • The permit is normally valid for up to three years or the contract/hosting duration if shorter; renewal can be possible.
  • Family applications may be submitted jointly or subsequently under the mobility framework.
  • After completion, a separate residence of up to 12 months may be available to seek suitable work or start a business.

Frequently asked questions

Recognition of Indian qualifications, working in English and moving with your family in Spain.

For ICT professionals

  • Most software, data, cloud, cybersecurity and general IT occupations are non-regulated in Spain; formal professional recognition is usually not required simply to practise the occupation.
  • Immigration evidence is separate: the Blue Card or national highly qualified route may require a degree, proof of level/comparability or documented professional experience.
  • If the role belongs to a regulated profession, formal recognition/homologation and any professional registration must be completed.
  • Check the route before starting a recognition process; experience-based ICT eligibility can sometimes be more relevant than academic homologation.

Indian document preparation

  • Keep degree certificates, transcripts and detailed experience letters consistent with the CV and proposed role.
  • Public documents may need an Apostille from the competent Indian authority and a sworn translation into Spanish or the relevant co-official language.
  • Experience letters should describe dates, hours/level, responsibilities and technologies—not only the job title.
  • Employers may be skills-first while the immigration route still requires formal documentary evidence.

English-only roles exist—but Spanish changes the opportunity set

There is no general Spanish-language requirement built into the EU Blue Card or Law 14/2013 highly qualified route. In practice, English is most viable in multinational product teams, start-ups, global delivery centres and research environments. Spanish is important across much of the wider market and is often decisive for client-facing, consulting, public-sector, local SME and management roles. In some regions, a co-official language may also be relevant. The EULGO academic sample likewise contained many Spanish-language or bilingual programmes and relatively few English-only records.

Family mobility depends on the principal route

Under the Law 14/2013 mobility framework, eligible family members of highly qualified professionals, Blue Card holders, researchers and intra-company transferees can generally apply jointly or subsequently. Family residence permits under this framework generally authorise employed or self-employed work. The general employed-worker route follows the ordinary family-reunification framework and may involve different timing and conditions. Always check the rules attached to the principal applicant’s specific permit.
