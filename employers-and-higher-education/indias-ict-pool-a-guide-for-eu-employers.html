India's ICT Pool: A Guide for EU Employers

A guide to India's ICT workforce, talent sources and recruitment practices for EU employers.

India's tech industry directly employs about 5.95 million people (FY26, April 2025–March 2026), the largest Information and Communication Technology (ICT) workforce outside the US and China. It is fed every year by one of the world's largest higher-education systems and a growing vocational skills network. For EU employers facing ICT shortages, or struggling to recruit for specialised roles, India is the biggest single source of skilled international talent.

Interest remains strong among Indian ICT talent, at all levels, to explore a move to Europe. According to Federation of European Business in India (FEBI) survey, more than 56 % of the European Business are eager to recruit from India.

Common skills

Indian ICT talent is strongest in enterprise software engineering, cloud and testing, built through decades of delivering projects for global clients. AI and data skills are growing fastest, while deep product, design and niche-domain skills are steadily growing..

Strengths EU employers usually report. English is the working language of the industry. Candidates are used to distributed teams, client-facing delivery, agile methods and process certifications (CMMI, ISO). Vendor certifications (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, SAP, Salesforce) are widespread and easy to verify.

Talent source 1: Higher Education Institutions (HEIs)

HEIs are the main source of graduate ("fresher") ICT talent, and campus placement is the most structured way to hire them. India has 58,000+ HEIs with 43.3 million students (AISHE 2021–22); about 3.7 million were enrolled in BE/BTech engineering degrees in 2020–21 (AISHE via Careers360). Almost half of engineering seats are in five southern and western states: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Maharashtra.

Tiers of institutions. Quality and cost of hiring vary sharply by tier.

The main degrees that graduates obtain are Bachelor of Engineering (BE)/Bachelor of Technology (BTech) – 4 years; Bachelor of Computer Applications (BCA) and Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (BSc Computer Science) – 3 years; Master of Computer Applications (MCA) – 2 years; and Master of Technology (MTech)/Master of Engineering (ME) – 2 years.Under the National Education Policy 2020, some universities now award 4-year honours bachelor's degrees, which can help with recognition in Europe.

Employers’ access to Indian talent: how “campus placement” works

Campus placement is an opportunity for employers to directly engage with Indian students, including the possibility to start the recruitment process, as per the following steps:

  1. Register with the placement cell. Each institution has a Training and Placement Office (T&P) that controls company access to its students.
  2. Pre-placement talk and job description. Employers discuss the job and what it involves, share expected salary range (Cost To Company), location and eligibility (for example, minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA), no active backlogs).
  3. Online assessment. Aptitude, coding and technical tests, often via platforms such as HackerRank, HackerEarth or Mettl.
  4. Interviews. Usually one or two technical rounds and an HR round, often on the same day.
  5. Offer. Offers are made on campus; most institutes enforce a "one student, one job" rule.
  6. Joining. Graduates join after final exams, usually June–August.

Timing. The main placement season runs from about August to December for students graduating the following summer. Internship hiring (which often converts to a pre-placement offer) runs earlier.

Other routes into HEIs. Paid internships (6 months is common in final year), hackathons, sponsored labs and research projects, and faculty partnerships. Erasmus+ and bilateral university links also let EU firms meet students who have studied in Europe.

Talent source 2: Skills institutions – the vocational training pathway

Skills institutions supply certified, job-ready technicians and entry-level ICT staff for support, networking, testing and junior development roles, often at lower cost and in larger numbers than universities. They sit under the oversign of Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship, Government of India (MSDE), separate from the university system.

Qualification framework. Skills courses are mapped to the National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF), levels 1–8. The National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET) approves qualifications and maintain a repository on a public domain https://www.kaushalverse.ncvet.gov.in/homepage/repository.

How to hire from skills institutions

  • Contact Directorate General of Training or individual ITIs or training-partner placement cells directly, or through state skill development missions.
  • Post vacancies on the Skill India Digital Hub JobX portal.
  • Co-design a short course with a training partner, then hire the cohort.
  • Use apprenticeships under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme to trial candidates before a permanent offer.

Talent source 3: Experienced professionals already in jobs

Most of India's ~5.95 million tech workers are employed, and mid-level and senior hires almost always come from this group as "lateral" hires. They are the fastest route to productive staff for EU roles.

Where they work. IT services firms (Tata Consultancy Services , Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Tech Mahindra, Cognizant, Accenture), global capability centres (GCCs) of multinationals, product companies and start-ups, and consulting firms. Main hubs: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Delhi NCR (Gurugram, Noida) and Mumbai.

Motivations. Beyond pay, experienced Indian professionals look for international exposure, more autonomy and ownership of their work than client-services work allows, a path to permanent residence for their family, and work-life balance. EU roles typically score well on these criteria, especially the last two, which is worth stating in job ads.

Where to find them. LinkedIn (very widely used), Naukri.com (India's largest job portal), Indeed, Instahyre, foundit, and specialist recruitment agencies. Employee referrals are a strong channel; many Indian firms pay referral bonuses.

How hiring typically works in India

A typical lateral ICT hire in India takes 3–6 weeks from first contact to signed offer, then 1–3 months of notice before joining. Hiring for an EU-based role adds visa and relocation time on top.

  1. 1

    Sourcing

    Job portals, LinkedIn, recruitment agencies (commonly paid 8–15% of annual CTC), referrals, campus drives and skilling-partner pipelines.

  2. 2

    Screening

    CV shortlist, then a recruiter call to confirm current CTC, expected CTC, notice period and location preference. These three numbers are asked openly in India and candidates expect the question.

  3. 3

    Assessment

    Online coding or aptitude test, then 2–3 technical interviews (often including system design for senior roles) and a managerial round.

  4. 4

    HR discussion

    Salary negotiation, usually anchored on current CTC. Employers may ask candidates to share payslips to justify salary expectations.

  5. 5

    Offer letter

    States CTC split into fixed pay, variable pay, allowances and benefits. Written, time-limited offers reduce dropout.

  6. 6

    Pre-joining

    Background verification, resignation, notice period and collection of relieving letters and degree certificates.

  7. 7

    Joining and onboarding

    Dropouts between offer and joining are a known risk, so regular contact during the notice period matters.

Hiring into the EU

The EU Blue Card is the main route for degree-holding Indian ICT professionals, and Indians already hold more Blue Cards than any other nationality (about 21,000). The revised Blue Card rules also let member states accept ICT specialists with at least 3 years of relevant experience insteadof a corresponding degree.

Routes for hiring into the EU
Route Who it suits Notes
EU Blue CardGraduates and experienced IT professionals with a job offer above the national salary thresholdThresholds differ by country and change yearly; check the national immigration site
National skilled-worker visasCandidates below Blue Card thresholds, or with vocational qualificationsFor example Germany's skilled-worker route and Opportunity Card, the Netherlands' highly skilled migrant scheme, France's Talent Passport
Intra-corporate transfer (ICT permit)Staff moving from an Indian entity of the same groupUseful for GCCs and IT services firms
India–EU FTA mobility provisionsBusiness visitors, contractual service suppliers, intra-corporate transfereesConcluded January 2026; operational details still emerging

India–EU mobility framework. Alongside the free trade agreement, India and the EU agreed a mobility pact covering skilled workers, students, researchers and seasonal workers (Intellinews). The EU is opening its first Legal Gateway Office in India as a single point of information on legal routes to member states (Business Standard).

Recognition of qualifications. Check whether the candidate's university is listed in your country's recognition database (for example, Germany's anabin) or get a statement of comparability. Indian degrees from recognised universities are generally accepted; vocational certificates need case-by-case assessment.
